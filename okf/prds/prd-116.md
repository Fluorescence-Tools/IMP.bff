# Extending the dye rotamer library

**Status:** in progress (see the 2026-08-27 section at the end)
**Done:** Stage 0 — the pipeline generates a library and reproduces `A48_C1R`
(overlap 0.892 against a measured ceiling of 0.815). Two of eleven new
libraries built and checked.
**Missing:** The other nine; stage 1 registry honesty; Cy3B and ATTO 647N,
which AMBER-DYES cannot express at all; ATTO 655, which no published force
field covers.
**Next:** finish the queue, then fetch the CHARMM-DYES deposit for Cy3B.


## Wanted, added 2026-09-07 (from PRD-136)

**Alexa488 on p-acetyl-phenylalanine (ketoxime, linker `P1R`).** Ten of the fifteen measured Alexa488 T4L sites —
the ones with residual anisotropy — use Alexa Fluor 488 hydroxylamine on pAcF, a chemistry no force field here
covers. Owner decision: parameterise it — and ticket `T-20260907-05` was resolved the same day without new work: AMBER-DYES
`B1R` already *is* the pAcF ketoxime label (verified by substructure match), `A48_B1R` is simulated and a shipped
`AlexaFluor 488 B1R` library exists. Nothing to generate; the consumer (`prototypes/dye_timewarp`) uses `B1R`.

## Why

The rotamer route needs a library entry per dye+linker. Six dyes are wanted.
Measured against `data/rotamer_library/` on 2026-08-19:

| wanted | registry name | files | state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cy3B | `Lumiprobe Cy3b C2R` → `Cy3b_C2R` | pdb + 3 bcif + 3 weights | **ships** |
| ATTO 488 | `ATTO 488 C3R` → `T48_C3R` | pdb + 3 bcif + 3 weights | **ships** |
| Alexa 647 | `AlexaFluor 647 C2R` / `L1R` → `A64_C2R`, `A64_L1R` | both complete | **ships** |
| Cy3 | `Lumiprobe Cy3 C2R` → `Cy3_C2R_HandyFRET` | **none** | declared, no data |
| sulfoCy5 | `Lumiprobe Cy5 C2R` → `Cy5_C2R_HandyFRET` | **none** | declared, no data — *and* sulfo-Cy5 is not Cy5 |
| ATTO 647N | — | — | **absent from the registry entirely** |

Three further facts fall out of the same check, and each is a defect in its own
right:

* **`Cy3_C2R` and `Cy5_C2R` are declared but empty.** `libraries.json` names
  them with a `_HandyFRET` filename; no `.pdb`, no `.bcif`, no weights ship.
  A user asking for `Lumiprobe Cy3 C2R` gets a file-not-found from inside the
  loader rather than "that library is not available", which is the difference
  between a missing feature and a broken one.
* **`C3N_C2R` ships and is undeclared.** A complete entry — pdb, three cutoffs,
  three weight files — that `libraries.json` does not name, so nothing can ask
  for it. 97 atoms, residue names `C3N` + `C2R`.
* **`Clusters_populations.csv` is wrong and unread.** 56 of its 93
  `(library, cutoff)` rows disagree with the number of weights actually
  shipped, in both directions (`A48_C1R_cutoff30`: table 32, file 33;
  `A35_L1R_cutoff20`: table 516, file 520). Nothing in `pyext/src`, the tests,
  the examples or `../chisurf` reads it. It is a stale artefact of the upstream
  build being shipped as if it were data.

## What a library entry is

Established by reading the shipped ones, not from documentation:

| part | example | content |
|---|---|---|
| topology | `A48_C1R.pdb` | one conformer, atom names and residue names — `A48` for the dye, `C1R` for the linker; the split drives `representation/rotamer.py:711` |
| conformers | `A48_C1R_cutoff{10,20,30}.bcif` | the ensemble, one frame per cluster representative, in the BinaryCIF `_atom_site` form PRD-113 moved to |
| weights | `A48_C1R_cutoff{N}_weights.txt` | one raw cluster population per conformer, one per line, `%.18e` |
| registry | `libraries.json` | `author`, `citation`, `licence`, `filename` (the *cutoff30* stem by convention), `mu` and `r` as MDAnalysis-style atom selections for the transition dipole and its reference point, `positive`/`negative` charge selections |

The three cutoffs are three clusterings of one trajectory, not three
trajectories: for `A48_C1R` they are 711, 123 and 33 conformers. The number in
the name is the leader-clustering RMSD threshold; **the units are not recorded
anywhere in this repository** and must be pinned before anything is generated,
because a generated library at the wrong threshold is indistinguishable from a
correct one until it is used.

## Provenance, and why generated entries are not the same kind of thing

Every shipped entry carries `author: D Montepietra, G Tesei, JM Martins,
MBA Kunze, RB Best, K Lindorff-Larsen`, `citation:
10.1038/s42003-024-05910-6`, `licence: GPLv3`. They are FRETpredict's, produced
by that group's protocol, and `test/references/cgdye_fretpredict_pins.json`
pins this repository's rotamer route against FRETpredict's own numbers on them.

An entry generated here is a different object with the same file layout. It
must say so in `author`/`citation`/`licence`, and the FRETpredict parity pins
must **not** be extended over it — there is nothing upstream to be at parity
with. This is the single most important thing for the PRD to get right: the
directory would otherwise come to hold two kinds of entry that look identical
and mean different things.

## What already exists to build with

| piece | where | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `LangevinDyeSampler` | `cgdye/sampling.py:339` | IMP Langevin/Brownian dynamics on the dye force field, protein as soft-sphere obstacles, anchor fixed |
| `LinkerSampler`, `generate_linker_rotamers` | `cgdye/sampling.py:516,682` | torsion-space Metropolis over the linker, then leader clustering — already returns a clustered library with transitions |
| `cluster_frames_leader` | `cgdye/sampling.py:48` | the leader clustering, RMSD without alignment |
| `rotamer_transition_matrix`, `rotamer_correlation_times` | `cgdye/sampling.py:102,121` | the kinetics FRETpredict's libraries do not carry |
| `bin/imp_bff_traj2bcif` | `bin/` | writes the conformer file in exactly the shipped format |
| `DyeForceFieldSystem` + `build_dye_protein_system` | C++ / `cgdye/topology.py` | the force field a dye is propagated on |

So the sampling and the file writing both exist. What does not exist is
anything that produces the *entry* — the four parts together, registered — and
no library in this repository has ever been generated.

## Blocked on

**[`impropers_are_dropped.md`](../validation/impropers_are_dropped.md).**
`build_dye_protein_system` hard-codes `impropers = []`, discarding 81 improper
terms across the two shipped components. For a dye whose ring feature is marked
`rb NO, md_fixed NO` — atto655's is — those impropers are the only term keeping
the chromophore planar. Generating a rotamer library under that force field
would sample a pyramidalising chromophore and bake the result into shipped
data, where the transition dipole is *defined* by the ring geometry. This is a
hard precondition, not a caveat.

## Stages

### Stage 0 — retire CX4 and atto655 as system fixtures

Both are from another project (user, 2026-08-19). They are currently the only
combined-system fixtures: `test/cgdye/test_combined_system.py`,
`test_cgdye_integration.py`, `test_topology.py`,
`expensive_test_all_dyes_sampling.py`, the new improper gate in
`test/scoring/test_scoring.py`, plus `cgdye/cli.py` and `analysis.py` defaults.

Retiring them means replacing the fixture, not deleting the tests — the
combined-system builder needs *a* dye+protein pair to be tested on. The
replacement should be a dye already in the rotamer library, so the fixture and
the library stop being disjoint worlds.

`atto655`'s mol2 and template survive this stage as a **candidate for the dye
library** (user: "consider adding atto655 to dye library"), not as a fixture.
It is the one dye in the repository with a hand-built force-field template, so
it is the natural first generated entry — see stage 3.

### Stage 1 — make the registry honest

Independent of any generation, and worth landing on its own:

* decide `Cy3_C2R` / `Cy5_C2R`: either the data ships or the declarations go.
  A declared-but-absent entry is worse than an absent one.
* **`Cy3b_C2R.pdb` is a copy-paste of `CF660R_C2R.pdb`** — byte-identical
  files, residue name `CF6` in both (found 2026-08-23 by the PRD-118
  `.drot` sweep: 93/95 libraries convert exactly, these two fail with
  10 Å template-vs-frames bond deviations while every other library sits at
  ≤ 0.02 Å; `prototypes/drot_rotlib/CONVERTING.md`). The Cy3b cutoff10
  trajectory carries real Cy3b conformers that cannot match a CF660R
  template — the entry needs an upstream-correct template or the data is
  unusable as a library.
* declare `C3N_C2R`, or move it out. Identify what it is first — 97 atoms,
  linker `C2R`; the name suggests a non-sulfonated Cy3, which is a claim to
  check against the atom composition rather than assume.
* delete `Clusters_populations.csv`, or regenerate it from the weights files
  and add the test that keeps it true. Nothing reads it, so the honest move is
  probably deletion.
* settle whether **sulfoCy5** is what `Lumiprobe Cy5 C2R` meant. Sulfonation
  changes charge and hydration and therefore the linker ensemble; if the user
  needs sulfo-Cy5 specifically, it is a generation target, not a naming fix.

### Stage 2 — the generation pipeline, as a prototype

In `prototypes/dye_library/` — a new directory; `prototypes/fast_label_score`
and `prototypes/quench_pinn` belong to another session and are not touched.

The pipeline is: force-field system for dye+linker → sampling → clustering at
three thresholds → the four entry parts. Two decisions it must make explicitly:

1. **Free dye or dye-on-protein.** FRETpredict's libraries are ensembles of the
   *free* dye+linker, screened against the protein at use time; that is what
   makes one library reusable across sites. `LangevinDyeSampler` is built for
   the on-protein case. Generating with obstacles present would produce a
   library that silently encodes one site's environment. The pipeline must
   sample free unless there is a stated reason not to.
2. **Which sampler.** `generate_linker_rotamers` (torsion-space Metropolis) and
   `LangevinDyeSampler` (Cartesian dynamics on the force field) are different
   ensembles. They should be compared on the same dye before one is chosen,
   because the choice is not obviously either way and it determines what the
   weights mean.

### Stage 3 — the parity bar, then the dyes

**The gate is regeneration, not plausibility.** Before any new dye:
regenerate `A48_C1R` — the entry the FRETpredict pins already cover — with the
pipeline, and compare against the shipped one. Not conformer-by-conformer,
which no stochastic method will reproduce, but on the observables the library
exists to produce: ⟨κ²⟩, the R_DA distribution, and the E_static/E_dyn1/E_dyn2
triple on the pinned structures, at a tolerance stated in advance.

A pipeline that cannot reproduce A48_C1R has no business producing ATTO 647N,
and the difference will not be visible by looking at the output.

Then, in order of what the repository can support:

1. **atto655** — has a template already; the shakedown case.
2. **ATTO 647N** — absent entirely; needs mol2 + template + parameters.
3. **Cy3**, **sulfoCy5** — needs the stage-1 naming decision first.

Each new dye needs a force-field template (`_cgdye_improper`, `_cgdye_feature`,
LJ types) built by hand, which is the real per-dye cost and is not automated by
anything here.

### Stage 4 — kinetics the shipped libraries do not carry

`rotamer_transition_matrix` and `rotamer_correlation_times` already exist and
FRETpredict's entries have nowhere to put their output. A generated entry could
carry the transition matrix and rotamer correlation time alongside the weights,
which is what a dynamic-quenching or diffusion-with-traps model needs and
currently has to assume. Additive — it does not change how existing entries
load — so it is last and separable.

## Not in scope

* Changing the rotamer route's physics or the FRETpredict pins.
* Retiring the FRETpredict libraries. They stay; generated entries sit
  alongside them, distinguishable by their registry metadata.
* Automating force-field parameterisation for arbitrary dyes.

## Where to pick this up

**2026-08-25 — the research is done, the MD is not, and it should not run
here.** Everything below is verified; the pipeline and the full account are in
[`prototypes/dye_library/README.md`](../../prototypes/dye_library/).

* **The blocker this PRD names is resolved.** `cutoff10/20/30` is a **minimum
  cluster population**, not an RMSD threshold -- FRETpredict's
  `rotamer_libraries.py` filters `clusters[clusters.N < cutoff]`, which is why
  cutoff10 yields *more* clusters than cutoff30. It no longer has to be
  guessed at before generating.
* **The parity gate needs no MD.** FRETpredict's raw 140.5 ns trajectory for
  A48_C1R (28110 frames at 5 ps) is kept in `junk/fretpredict_protocol/`, so
  stage 3's "regenerate an entry that already exists" can be run against their
  own input. That is the first move, and it is cheap.
* **Parameters, and which distribution.** AMBER-DYES, taken from **AmberTools**
  (`leaprc.amberdyes`) rather than the GROMACS release: the two disagree about
  the linker, and only AmberTools has the saturated thiosuccinimide that a
  maleimide dye forms with a cysteine thiol (37 atoms for C1R against 35).
  Building it as `ACE - <linker> - NME` with the dye on `N99` reproduces the
  shipped template exactly -- 83 atoms, name for name, charge -2.000.
* **The dyes this unlocks.** Eleven internal-attachment libraries AMBER-DYES
  supports and this package does not ship, **sulfo-Cy5 (`C5W_L1R`) among
  them** -- which settles this PRD's "sulfoCy5 is not Cy5" item: it is a
  distinct residue that exists upstream, not a naming fix.
* **Not on this machine.** ~106 ns/day in explicit water, a day and a half per
  library, on hardware shared with other work. GBn2 implicit solvent is ~20x
  cheaper and `compare_to_shipped.py` is written to test whether its ensemble
  is equivalent, but the MD belongs on a box with a real GPU. Note that
  **GROMACS cannot run these systems at all** (AMBER-DYES uses per-pair 1-4
  scaling, which ParmEd refuses to convert); it is OpenMM from the Amber
  prmtop.

### Cy3B: the shipped entry is unusable and cannot be regenerated here

Verified 2026-08-27 by reading the shipped containers:

* `Cy3b_C2R_cutoff10` — 505 conformers, **137 atoms, every one of them residue
  `CF6`**
* `CF660R_C2R_cutoff10` — 1008 conformers, **137 atoms, every one `CF6`**

Two defects, not one. The template is the wrong molecule (this PRD's

**It cannot be regenerated with the current pipeline.** AMBER-DYES has no
Cy3B: the distribution ships `Cy_3`, `Cy_5`, `Cy_5_Water`, `Cy_5.5`, `Cy_7`,
`Cy_7.5`, `Cy3_Water` and no Cy3B. Cy3B is not a Cy3 variant that can be
approximated by one either — its polymethine bridge is conformationally locked
by a fused rigid ring, which is the entire point of the dye and precisely what
a rotamer library measures.

### The CHARMM-DYES route — what it covers, read from the paper

Shaw, Johnston-Wood, Ambrose, Craggs & Hill, *JCTC* **16**(12):7817-7824 (2020),
[10.1021/acs.jctc.0c00721](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.0c00721), open
access CC BY. Parameters deposited at Sheffield ORDA
[12593681](https://orda.shef.ac.uk/articles/dataset/CHARMM-DYES_Fluorescent_dye_parameters_for_use_with_the_CHARMM_forcefield/12593681),
in **GROMACS format**.

Section 2.1 names the parameterised set exactly:

> "we present parameters for Alexa Fluor 647, Atto 550, Atto 647N, Cy3, Cy3B,
> Cy5, and Cy7"

| dye | CHARMM-DYES | AMBER-DYES |
|---|---|---|
| **Cy3B** | **yes** | no |
| **ATTO 647N** | **yes** | no |
| ATTO 550 | yes | no (AMBER has ATTO 514, not 550) |
| Alexa 647, Cy3, Cy5, Cy7 | yes | yes — already covered |
| **ATTO 655** | **no** | **no** |

The paper is explicit that the linker coverage is a *subset*, not a matrix:
"Providing parameters for every possible combination of dye, linker, and
attachment point would require considerable effort... we restrict our
attention here to a representative subset, which can easily be extended by
analogy." So the deposited files must be inspected for which attachment
chemistry is actually present before any generation is planned — the
`C2R`-equivalent may have to be built by analogy.

### What has to be done for Cy3B

1. **Fetch and inspect** the ORDA deposit (12593681). Confirm the licence on
   the *dataset* (the article is CC BY; the deposit was not readable without a
   browser session) and record which linker/attachment residues ship with
   Cy3B. Vendor under `junk/` like `amber-dyes`, never into `data/`.
2. **Decide the engine.** The files are GROMACS-format CHARMM. This is the
   mirror image of the AMBER-DYES problem and lands better: OpenMM reads
   GROMACS topologies (`GromacsTopFile`) and CHARMM `psf`/`rtf`/`prm`
   directly, so `make_library.py` keeps its OpenMM production path and only
   its build step changes. **Do not** attempt the AMBER route — that failure
   is already recorded (ParmEd refuses AMBER-DYES' per-pair 1-4 scaling).
3. **Build the labelled cysteine** as the AMBER path does: cap it, bond the
   dye to the linker explicitly, and *verify the topology before spending GPU
   time*. The unbonded-dye trap cost a 13.8 h run and was invisible in every
   log; `verify_topology()` exists and must be given a CHARMM equivalent.
4. **Re-derive the parity bar.** `A48_C1R` cannot gate a CHARMM run — the
   force fields differ. Either gate on a dye that both force fields cover
   (Cy3, Cy5, Alexa 647 are in both) by generating it under CHARMM and
   comparing to the AMBER-generated one, or accept that the CHARMM libraries
   carry a different provenance and say so in their `drot.provenance` record.
   **The first is much stronger and is cheap** — it is one extra run, and it
   is the only way to learn whether the two force fields even agree about a
   linker ensemble.
5. **Delete or replace `Cy3b_C2R`.** It cannot stay as it is. Until a
   replacement exists, the honest state is "absent", which also fixes the
   registry lie.

## 2026-08-27b — documentation is a validity condition, and the ATTO 655 route

### A library that cannot say how it was made is invalid

Stated as a requirement, not a nicety: **a rotamer library without full
provenance is not usable as evidence.** Two libraries of the same dye can
differ in conformer count, in ⟨R_DA⟩ and in κ² because of the water model, the
non-bonded cutoff, the timestep, the length of the run or the clustering
metric — and *none of that is recoverable from the coordinates*. A library that
does not carry those facts cannot be checked, reproduced, or compared against
another, which is what a library is for.

This is now enforced rather than encouraged:

* `make_library.py` writes `<stem>_md.json` beside every trajectory **before
  the production run starts** — force field and its citation, water model,
  cutoff, box rule, ions, integrator, temperature, friction, timestep,
  constraints, hydrogen mass, constraint tolerance, barostat, equilibration and
  production lengths, frame interval, start structure (seeded vs compacted),
  which atoms were recorded, engine, engine version, platform. 30 keys.
* `cluster_dihedral.py` **refuses to write a library** when that file is
  missing, rather than producing one whose provenance describes the clustering
  of an unknown simulation. That is worse than describing nothing.
* The record written into the `.drot.pto` is the **whole chain in one
  operation**: `settings_json` carries the MD record nested under `md` plus the
  13 clustering settings, so `settings_hash` changes when *either* half
  changes. Alongside it: an `mfdb_edge_tags` link to the trajectory pinned by
  **SHA-256** (not a path — the trajectories live on a machine that may not
  exist next year), and `mfdb_attribution_tags` with author, citation, licence
  (`GPL-3.0-only`, matching what the shipped libraries declare, since the
  clustering protocol is FRETpredict's and is GPLv3) and `redistributed_via`.
  15 tags in total.
* Trajectories that predate this get `md_record_from_log.py`, which
  reconstructs what is genuinely recoverable from the run log and stamps
  `reconstructed_from_log: true`. A reconstruction that hides that it is one
  would be worse than no record.

`mfdb_attribution_tags` refuses an artifact with neither licence nor terms —
"data whose terms are unknown is not the same as data that is unrestricted" —
which is the same discipline applied one level down, and it caught this
omission rather than letting it ship.

### ATTO 655: parameterize → MD → rotamer

ATTO 655 is in neither AMBER-DYES nor CHARMM-DYES, so the pipeline gains a
stage it has never had. What exists in the repo today is
`data/cgprobe/inputs/structures/atto655.mol2` (70 atoms) and a coarse-grained
template — a **structure with no MD parameters behind it**.

**There are webservers, and the honest answer is that they are a starting point
and not the deliverable.** The relevant ones:

| route | force field | server | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CGenFF / ParamChem** | CHARMM (CGenFF) | [cgenff.silcsbio.com](https://cgenff.silcsbio.com/), free for academics | the natural choice — it is what CHARMM-DYES itself started from, and it pairs with the Cy3B/ATTO 647N work |
| SwissParam | CHARMM-compatible | [swissparam.ch](https://www.swissparam.ch/) | bonded terms from MMFF, non-bonded from CHARMM — a different provenance to reason about |
| ATB | GROMOS | [atb.uq.edu.au](https://atb.uq.edu.au/) | wrong force-field family for this stack |
| LigParGen | OPLS-AA | [zarbi.chem.yale.edu/ligpargen](http://zarbi.chem.yale.edu/ligpargen/) | wrong family; 1.14*CM1A charges |
| Antechamber + GAFF/AM1-BCC | AMBER | local, no server | matches the existing AMBER path; AM1-BCC charges are the weak point |

**The trap is documented by the CHARMM-DYES paper itself**, and it is the
single most important fact here. Shaw et al. ran exactly this route for their
dyes and reported that CGenFF's own confidence heuristic "indicated a strong
need for reparameterization of almost all of the bonded parameters", concluding
that "due to the high anisotropy of the electron density, automatic generation
of parameters by analogy using standard tools does not provide sufficient
accuracy". CGenFF's documented thresholds say the same thing quantitatively:
penalties 10–50 need validation, above 50 need optimisation. A large
polycyclic dye will produce penalties in the second band for most of its
bonded terms.

So the route is:

1. **Structure and protonation.** Start from `atto655.mol2`; confirm the
   protonation state and net charge at pH 7 against the vendor structure.
   Decide the linker: ATTO 655 is used as a maleimide, so the target is the
   reacted thiosuccinimide, as for the AMBER dyes.
2. **CGenFF via the server** for atom types and initial bonded/charge
   parameters. **Record every penalty.** This is the documentation gate: a
   parameter set whose penalties are unrecorded cannot be defended later.
3. **QM reference.** Optimise at DFT (the CHARMM-DYES protocol used DF-MP2 for
   dipoles); derive charges by RESP/ESP rather than trusting analogy, and
   compute the relaxed dihedral scans for every rotatable bond that will carry
   a rotamer.
4. **Reparameterize** the bonded terms whose penalty exceeds ~50 against those
   scans, and the charges against the QM ESP. This is the step that is a
   project rather than a task.
5. **Validate before any production MD**: reproduce the QM dipole magnitude and
   direction (κ² depends on direction, so this is not optional), the planarity
   of the chromophore, and the dihedral profiles.
6. **Then** the existing pipeline runs unchanged: `verify_topology` →
   140 ns → `check_new_library.py` → `cluster_dihedral.py`, with the CGenFF
   penalties and the QM level of theory carried in `md_record` so the library
   states that its parameters were derived here and how.

**Sequencing.** Do Cy3B and ATTO 647N first (CHARMM-DYES, parameters already
published and validated), because that work builds the CHARMM branch of the
build step that ATTO 655 will then reuse. Attempting ATTO 655 first means
writing a parameterisation project *and* a new force-field path at once, with
nothing to validate the second against.

## 2026-08-27c — CHARMM-DYES fetched; what Cy3B and ATTO 655 actually need

### The deposit is in hand

`junk/charmm-dyes/` (vendored, not in `data/`). ORDA 12593681, **CC BY 4.0**,
`forcefield.zip` md5 `4670a7fca38fe90f17811121d860fde5` — verified against the
figshare record. GROMACS format, which suits us: OpenMM reads GROMACS
topologies, so the production half of `make_library.py` is unchanged.

Residues are named dye / dye+**L**inker / dye+**T**hymine:

| dye | alone | +linker | +thymine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alexa 647 | `AX6` | `X6L` | `X6T` |
| ATTO 550 | `AT5` | `T5L` | `T5T` |
| ATTO 647N | `AT6` | `T6L` | `T6T` |
| Cy3 | `CY3` | `Y3L` | `Y3T` |
| **Cy3B** | **`C3B`** | **`YBL`** | `YBT` |
| Cy5 | `CY5` | `Y5L` | `Y5T` |
| Cy7 | `CY7` | `Y7L` | `Y7T` |

**ATTO 655 is not among them**, confirming the paper: only ATTO 550 and
ATTO 647N.

### The linker they ship is the wrong chemistry for us

This is the finding that decides the work. CHARMM-DYES' `LNK` residue is
**C13 H23 N2 O2 — no sulfur at all**. It is a diamide alkyl spacer for
attaching a dye to *thymine*, which is what their `_T` residues are for. Every
library in this package is a **protein cysteine conjugate**: the AMBER `C1R`
linker is `CA HA C O C6 H10 H11 S1 C7 C8 H12 C9 O3 N3 C10 O4 C11...C15 N99` —
a thiosuccinimide ring (`S1`, `C7`, `C8`, `C9=O3`, `N3`, `C10=O4`) formed by
Michael addition of the cysteine thiol to the maleimide, then an alkyl spacer
to the amide `N99` that carries the dye.

So the `C3B` dye parameters are usable and the `YBL` linker is not. A Cy3B
library for cysteine labelling needs a maleimide-cysteine linker
**parameterized in CHARMM** — the AMBER `C1R`/`C2R` parameters cannot be
borrowed across force fields.

### Manufacturer chemistry, which is what the linker must reproduce

* **Cy3B maleimide** — Lumiprobe [Cyanine3B maleimide](https://www.lumiprobe.com/p/cy3b-maleimide),
  CAS **926905-86-4**. The published SMILES shows the spacer explicitly:
  `O=C1C=CC(N1CCNC(=O)C...)=O` — maleimide N, an **ethylenediamine** bridge
  (`N-CH2CH2-NH`), then an amide to the dye's acetyl arm; the dye carries a
  sulfonate. Reacted with cysteine this gives thiosuccinimide + `-CH2CH2-`
  amide, i.e. a **shorter spacer than `C1R`'s C5 chain** — which is exactly the
  sort of difference that moves ⟨R_DA⟩ and must not be approximated.
* **ATTO 655 maleimide** — ATTO-TEC; the free acid is
  [PubChem CID 16218785](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/16218785),
  **C27H33N3O6S, MW 527.6**,
  `CC[N+]1=C2C=C3C(=NC4=C(O3)C=C5C(=C4)C(CC(N5CCCC(=O)O)(C)C)CS(=O)(=O)[O-])C=C2CCC1`.
  The `CCCC(=O)O` is the carboxyl that becomes the maleimide reagent through an
  amide to a maleimide-amine; **the vendor datasheet is needed for the exact
  spacer** — it is not derivable from the free-acid structure, and guessing it
  would silently change the linker length.

### Answering "can these be done now"

**Cy3B — not yet, but the remaining work is now well defined.** Parameters in
hand. What is missing is one residue: the maleimide-cysteine linker in CHARMM,
built to Lumiprobe's ethylenediamine spacer, plus its junction to `C3B`. That
means CGenFF atom types and charges for a thiosuccinimide-plus-short-amide
fragment, checked against the existing `LNK`/protein types it borders. It is a
day of careful work, not a project — but it is not zero, and it needs the
CGenFF server, which requires an academic account that only the owner can
register.

**ATTO 655 — no.** No published parameters in either force field. It needs the
full route recorded in the previous section: CGenFF types, QM (DFT geometry,
RESP/ESP charges, relaxed dihedral scans), reparameterisation of every bonded
term above CGenFF penalty ~50, and validation against the QM dipole *direction*
before a single ns of production. That is a parameterisation project with its
own validation, not a run of the existing pipeline.

**Sequence.** Cy3B first — it builds the CHARMM branch of the build step and
the maleimide-cysteine linker residue that ATTO 655 will then reuse. ATTO 647N
and ATTO 550 come almost free once that exists, since their parameters are in
the same deposit. ATTO 655 last, when the only new thing is the dye itself.

## 2026-08-27d — Cy3B: feasibility settled, and the one problem that remains

Worked through the deposit rather than reasoning about it. Three findings, in
increasing order of how much they cost.

**1. The dye is ready to use.** `C3B` (Cy3B alone) and `YBL` (Cy3B + their
linker, 116 atoms) are complete residues with charges, and `ffdyesbonded.itp`
carries their bonded terms. Nothing about the chromophore needs doing.

**2. The thiosuccinimide ring is buildable from stock CGenFF.** Every type a
maleimide-cysteine adduct needs is already in the base force field shipped in
the same directory — `SG311` (thioether S), `CG2R53` (five-ring carbonyl C),
`NG2R53` (five-ring imide N), `OG2D1`, `CG321`, `NG2S1` — and each appears in
*existing residues* (`12A`, `26A`, `2MSA`, …), so their bonded parameters are
present and have been used before. The ring itself needs no new parameters and
no server.

**3. The junction is the real work, and it is small but not zero.**
CHARMM-DYES defined **809 custom atom types**, and their linker uses them too:
`CDC21`, `CA201` (amide C), `NS21` (amide N), `OD21` (amide O), `CA321`
(alkyl). So a linker built from CGenFF types does not meet a dye built from
CHARMM-DYES types anywhere that a bonded parameter exists. Whichever way the
boundary is drawn, a handful of terms cross it — one bond, ~4 angles, ~6-9
dihedrals.

The cheapest correct boundary is **not** at the dye: keep `C33-C34-C35(=O6)-N4`
— the first amide, which belongs to the dye's own arm and is fully
parameterized by CHARMM-DYES — and replace only what is distal to it with the
thiosuccinimide built in CGenFF. Then the crossing is a single aliphatic C-C
bond (`CA321`-`CG321`), and its bond/angle/dihedral terms can be taken by
analogy from the `CA321`-`CA321` terms CHARMM-DYES already provides, which is a
defensible transfer between two sp3 carbons of nearly identical environment —
and one that must be *recorded as a transfer*, not passed off as derived.

### The chemistry to reproduce

Lumiprobe Cy3B maleimide (CAS 926905-86-4): maleimide-N-`CH2CH2`-NH-C(=O)-
dye arm. Reacted with a cysteine thiol this gives, from the protein outward:

    CB - SG - [C3 ring: CH-CH2-C(=O)-N-C(=O)] - N-CH2CH2-NH - C(=O) - dye

which is **shorter than the AMBER `C1R` linker** (whose spacer is C11-C15, five
carbons). The library will therefore not be interchangeable with a `C1R`
library of the same dye, and the difference is real rather than a modelling
artefact.

### What is blocked and what is not

* **Not blocked:** building the residue, the ring, and the topology; the whole
  production half of the pipeline (OpenMM reads GROMACS topologies directly).
* **Blocked on judgement, not access:** the junction transfer above wants a
  check — a short vacuum or implicit-solvent run of the linker fragment alone,
  confirming the thiosuccinimide stays planar-amide and the ring does not
  distort, before 140 ns is spent on it.
* **Not needed after all:** the CGenFF server. The types and their parameters
  are already in the deposit. It would still be the right tool if the transfer
  above turns out not to hold.

### ATTO 655 — searched, and there is nothing

Searched for deposited parameters (GROMACS/AMBER/CHARMM topologies, supporting
information, ATB, Ligandbook) and for the MD literature. ATTO 655 is heavily
studied as *the* PET-quenching dye — Sauer, Doose and Neuweiler's
tryptophan-quenching work, and a 2026 bioRxiv on its quenching rate — but no
publication found deposits a reusable parameter set, and it is absent from both
AMBER-DYES and CHARMM-DYES.

The repository's own `data/cgprobe/inputs/structures/atto655.mol2` is
**`NO_CHARGES`** — 70 atoms, all charges 0.0000, residue `DDS`. Seventy atoms
matches the free acid `C27H33N3O6S` (MW 527.6,
[PubChem 16218785](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/16218785))
exactly, so it is a usable *starting geometry* and nothing more.

Manufacturer chemistry for the eventual build: ATTO-TEC sells ATTO 655
maleimide, and Jena Bioscience sells it as a thiol protein-labelling kit; the
free acid's `CCCC(=O)O` becomes the maleimide through an amide to a
maleimide-amine, but **the spacer length must come from the vendor datasheet**
— it is not derivable from the free acid, and assuming it would silently change
the linker length, which is the one thing a rotamer library is most sensitive
to.

So ATTO 655 remains what the previous section said: a parameterisation project.
Cy3B is now a bounded task.

### Three structure errors, caught and corrected

**PubChem's "Alexa Fluor 647 maleimide" (CID 177794446) is wrong.** It carries
two sulfoPROPYL chains, [M-H]- 965.21. The validated structure is
[M-H]- 979.2176 -- one CH2 heavier, i.e. one propyl and one BUTYL. This is
exactly the "conflicting reports on the linker length connecting the two
sulphonated SO3- groups (3-carbon or 4-carbon)" that Gebhardt et al. set out to
settle, and PubChem carries the losing side. Anything built on that scaffold
unchanged inherits the error.

**My own Alexa 555 was wrong, twice over.** First I built it on the bad PubChem
scaffold and compensated with a CH2 in the linker -- right formula, wrong
molecule. Then I "corrected" it to a sulfobutyl on the evidence of the mass
alone. **A formula cannot distinguish isomers**, and the NMR structure shows
Alexa 555 has the linker on the C3 quaternary carbon with sulfoPROPYL on both
nitrogens, while AF555 has the linker on the N+ with a sulfoBUTYL on C3. Those
are different topologies, which is the entire point of that comparison. The
labels already fitted from the wrong structure (`A55_C2R`, `A55_L1R`,
`A55_B1R`) were deleted and refitted.

**ATTO 550 is a xanthylium, not a rhodamine.** The positive charge sits on the
bridging ring OXYGEN and both tetrahydroquinoline nitrogens are neutral NH.
Every automated derivation failed for this reason -- antechamber perceives the
connectivity correctly from the CHARMM-DYES coordinates (85 atoms) but its
aromatic bond orders will not kekulise, and every attempt to place the charge
on a nitrogen was chemically wrong. Note also a **one-CH2 disagreement**: the
vendor drawing has a butanoic arm (C37H44N3O4), CHARMM-DYES' AT5 is propanoic
(C36H42N3O4). The vendor structure is used; linker length moves R_DA, so
libraries built against a CHARMM-DYES ATTO 550 are of a slightly different
molecule.

### BODIPY: solved to one remaining obstacle

Four separate blockers, three now solved:

1. **GAFF has no boron atom type.** Solved: a complete GAFF-compatible boron
   parameter set for exactly the BODIPY environment (four-coordinate B, two F,
   two aromatic N) is published at
   `github.com/Liu-group/Workflow-for-Umbrella-Sampling`,
   `Example/system1.frcmod` -- mass, bonds, angles, 9 dihedrals, impropers and
   Lennard-Jones. Vendored as `prototypes/dye_library/boron.frcmod` and loaded
   via `parmchk2 -afrc` and in the validating tleap build. **It is not from a
   dye paper**; a built BODIPY geometry should be checked before trusting a
   library made with it.
2. **sqm has no AM1 parameters for boron** ("There are no AM1 parameters for
   this element. Sorry."), and none for PM3 either -- both verified by running
   all three. **PM6 works.** Boron-bearing dyes are routed through PM6 via
   `-ek`. This is a real difference in charge model: BODIPY charges are
   PM6-derived where every other dye here is AM1-BCC, and a library built from
   them must say so.
3. **The zwitterion read as +1.** The formal-charge recovery had no rule for
   boron. Neutral boron is trivalent, so a four-coordinate B is anionic; the
   rule was added and the dye unit now passes.
4. **UNRESOLVED: the full label will not converge.** The dye alone converges
   under PM6, but the label (dye + C2R linker + caps, ~130 atoms) fails to
   reach SCF convergence in 1000 DIIS steps at 1.d-10, 1.d-8, 1.d-6 or 1.d-4.
   Loosening does not help, so it is oscillating rather than creeping. Options,
   none yet taken: charge the dye unit alone under PM6 and take linker charges
   from the nine dyes already fitted (most likely to work, but a different
   charge model that must be recorded); a different charge scheme (`-c gas`, or
   RESP from an external QM); or different SCF damping.

### HandyFRET: validation only

`external/handyfret/` holds five rotamer libraries from
<https://karri.anu.edu.au/handy/rl.html>. **"Copyright (c) 2014. All Rights
Reserved" -- no licence is granted**, so nothing from them, and nothing derived
from them, may ship. They are kept to check our own libraries against an
independent construction, and because they are grid-enumerated
sterically-allowed sets rather than Boltzmann-sampled ensembles they answer a
different question anyway. The `_HandyFRET` suffixes in `libraries.json` should
go: those entries get satisfied by our own simulations.

Their `.dat` format is undocumented and was decoded here: columns 0..n-3 are
dihedrals in degrees, column -2 is a weight normalised to exactly 1.000, column
-1 an index. **CY3 has 1591 non-zero weights against 523 conformers in its
DCD** -- they cannot be paired by order, so anyone using it must establish the
correspondence first.

## 2026-09-01 — BODIPY unblocked: the spliced charge model, and two gates keyed on a name

The BODIPY family is no longer blocked. All **15 labels** (BDP FL, BDP 558/568,
BDP R6G, BDP TMR, BDP TR × C2R/L1R/B1R) are parameterised, validated, and
queued for MD.

### What was actually blocking it

Not the boron parameters — `boron.frcmod` had covered those since 2026-08-31.
The block was that **no whole-label charge fit converges** for a boron-bearing
label:

| route | outcome |
| --- | --- |
| AM1 (`sqm` default) | no boron parameters at all |
| PM3 | no boron parameters at all |
| PM6, `scfconv` 1.d-10 … 1.d-4 | converges on the dye alone, never on the ~130-atom label |
| B3LYP/6-31G(d) RESP, Psi4 defaults | fails after 100 iterations |
| same, SAD guess + SOSCF + damping + 500 iterations | ~4 h per label, 2 of 3 failed |

The DFT route was killed after the second failure. Five methods is enough
evidence that the label is not going to converge as a unit.

### The route taken

The dye alone converges under PM6, and the linker is ordinary chemistry that
twelve other dyes have already been fitted with. So `splice_charges.py`
assembles each label from the two fits that do converge: **dye charges** from
that dye's own PM6/BCC unit fit, **linker and cap charges** from a fitted label
carrying the same linker (`T53_<linker>` for all three, so the linker charges
come from one molecule). Atom types travel with the charges from the same
sources — the staged label's linker carries placeholder types (`cG`, `hG`,
`sS`) that parmchk2 refuses, and a charge is only meaningful beside the type it
was fitted with. The residual (−0.08 to −0.21 e, the charge the stripped cap
took) is spread evenly over the linker at ≤0.005 e per atom, on the borrowed
half rather than the computed one.

All 15 pass parmchk2 with no ATTN terms, the tleap `check`, `saveamberparm`,
and `verify_topology` through the production MD code path.

### The model is mixed, and every library says so on its own

This matters more than the result. **`without full documentation, the rotamer
lib is invalid`** — so the caveat is carried by the data, not by this page:

* `splice_charges.py` writes `labels/<stem>.charges.json`: method, source and
  caveat per fragment, plus the residual and the reason no whole-label fit
  exists.
* `parameterize_dye.py` now writes the same file when it *fits* a label,
  recording the actual `argv` — so fitted and spliced labels are
  distinguishable by evidence rather than by memory.
* `make_library.py` reads it into the MD record as `charge_model`, alongside
  `force_field`, which the clustering step already refuses to write a library
  without.
* The 12 dyes fitted before that file existed were backfilled by
  `backfill_charge_records.py` from the `&qmmm` namelist their fit actually ran
  with (`param/<stem>/sqm.in`), every record marked `reconstructed: true`.

51 labels, 51 charge records: 36 uniform AM1-BCC, 15 spliced.

**Stated plainly:** the linker charges were fitted in the presence of a
different dye. Defensible — identical chemistry, near-identical environment in
all 15 labels, and the alternative is no BODIPY library at all — but *not*
equivalent to a whole-label fit. A BODIPY library must be reported as a mixed
charge model, never listed silently beside the AM1-BCC ones.

### Two bugs worth not repeating

Both were gates keyed on a **residue code** rather than on the molecule:

1. `validate_label` selected `boron.frcmod` with `code.startswith("BD")`. Of
   the five boron dyes only `BDF` matches — `B56`, `BR6`, `BTM` and `BTR` were
   validating with **no boron parameters loaded at all**.
2. `make_library.py` never loaded `boron.frcmod` in the MD build. parmchk2
   passes only the one improper it invents into the label's own frcmod; the
   mass, bond, angle, dihedral and van der Waals boron terms exist nowhere
   else. Every BODIPY MD would have died at the build.

Both now test the molecule — the formula, or the label's own atom types.
**Do not gate chemistry on a residue code.** This is the same class of error as
the hard-coded `leaprc.amberdyes` in the MD record: a claim standing in for a
check.

A third, unrelated: every file in `param/labels_src/` carries a field of
uninitialised bytes between the residue name and the charge, on exactly the dye
rows. Structural columns are intact and the fitted labels are clean, so it
never reached a parameter — the splice parses past it and writes a canonical
ATOM block. Worth fixing at the source in `label_structure`.

### MD campaign

BODIPY MD is queued as `run_queue_bodipy.sh`, two lanes chained behind the
running campaign, one per GPU. Two things differ from every other dye and are
recorded per run:

* **no seed** — there is no shipped BODIPY ensemble to start from, so these
  start from vacuum compaction and replicas differ by velocity seed alone.
  `start_structure` in the MD record says so.
* **spliced charges** — `charge_model` in the MD record says so.

Jobs are claimed through an atomic `mkdir` lock, so lanes self-balance as cards
free. The list runs replica 0 for all 15 labels before replica 1, so a partial
campaign covers every label rather than finishing a few.

45 runs × ~5.2 h at ~700 ns/day ≈ **234 GPU-hours, ~5 days on two cards**,
behind ~42 h of the existing T65 lane.

Existing campaign at this point: **18 of 20 runs complete**; `AF4_B1R_r2`
(139/150 ns) and `T65_C2R_r1` (95/150 ns) still running.

### 2026-09-01b — the force-field record was wrong on every new-dye run

Found while checking the first BODIPY run's MD record. All 21 completed
new-dye runs recorded

    force_field: [leaprc.protein.ff14SB, leaprc.water.tip3p, leaprc.amberdyes]

and cited the AMBER-DYES paper in `force_field_source`. **Not one of those
builds sources `leaprc.amberdyes`** — zero of 21 `build.in` files mention it.
They load GAFF2 and a per-label frcmod. The record named a force field the runs
did not use.

Two causes, both the same mistake in different places:

1. `_sourced_leaprcs` read only the `source` lines. The GAFF2 route *inlines*
   its leaprc (so the build does not depend on the env's search path), so 125
   lines of GAFF2 were invisible to it, as was every `loadamberparams` — the
   label's frcmod, and `boron.frcmod`.
2. `force_field_source` was a fixed string naming AMBER-DYES, emitted on every
   run regardless of what was loaded.

With `_sourced_leaprcs` reporting only tip3p, whatever wrote these records fell
back to the old hard-coded list. This is the **third** appearance of the same
failure — after the hard-coded `leaprc.amberdyes` in the MD record, and the two
boron gates keyed on `code.startswith("BD")`. A claim standing in for a check,
and one that looks checked, which is worse than a blank.

**Fixed.** Both fields are now derived from the run's own `build.in`:
`_sourced_leaprcs` reports the sourced leaprcs, the inlined one (identified by
the header tleap's leaprc files carry) and every `loadamberparams`;
`_force_field_source` emits citations for what was actually loaded — GAFF2 and
AM1-BCC for these, AMBER-DYES only when `leaprc.amberdyes` is present, plus the
boron source and TIP3P.

**Corrected retroactively** by `backfill_force_field.py`, from each run's own
`build.in`: 21 records on heinzehub, and the 7 affected runs in the SD1TB
archive (`runs/`, plus the `force_field` column of `MANIFEST.tsv`). The
original values are kept as `force_field_AS_ORIGINALLY_RECORDED` and
`force_field_source_AS_ORIGINALLY_RECORDED` with a `force_field_correction`
note, so the correction is visible rather than silent. **The builds themselves
were always right — this corrects the record of them, not the runs.** The 12

### 2026-09-02 — label-major queueing: a label with one replica is not a library

The BODIPY and resample queues ran **replica-major** — replica 0 for all 15
labels, then replica 1, then replica 2 — on the reasoning that a partial
campaign should cover every label rather than finishing a few. That was the
wrong trade, and the user called it: *"better have finished jobs, than a bunch
of unfinished."*

It is wrong because of the gate. Convergence is agreement **between**
independent replicas; a single replica can only be scored on halves of itself,
which share a starting basin and can agree while the ensemble is badly
under-sampled — `audit_convergence.py` reports that as *provisional*, not as a
pass. So replica-major would have produced, after five days of GPU time,
**fifteen labels that could not be shipped instead of seven that could**.

Both lanes are now label-major (`run_bodipy.sh`, `run_resample.sh` — new files,
because editing a running shell script makes bash re-read it mid-flight and
die, which already cost a queue once). Every ~15.6 h one more BODIPY label is
genuinely finished: three independent replicas, a real between-replica score, a
library that can be written and defended.

**A race this exposed.** Retiring the old lane while it held a job left its
claimed job running, and the new lane immediately claimed another onto the same
card — GPU 1 briefly ran two jobs at once. The order that avoids it: retire the
wrapper *first*, then stop the child, then release its lock; a wrapper killed
while its child runs will otherwise release the lock and claim again. Killing a
wrapper is safe for completion detection either way, because the completion
marker is printed by `make_library.py` itself, not by the queue. Resolved by
keeping the job with 19 ns invested (`BDF_B1R`) and dropping the one with no
production frames (`BDF_C2R_r1`), then chaining the lane behind it.

## 2026-09-03c — the ring-opened maleimide linkers

A maleimide plus a cysteine thiol gives a thiosuccinimide — the closed
five-membered ring every AMBER-DYES `*R` linker models. That ring is not the
end state: it hydrolyses to the succinamic-acid thioether, the **stable
hydrolyzate**, and it opens at **either** carbonyl, so each closed linker has
two regioisomers. A label old enough to be measured is substantially the open
form, which has one more rotatable bond, a carboxylate, and a different reach.
**AMBER-DYES ships only the closed ring.**

`prototypes/dye_library/open_linkers.py` derives the open residues from the
closed ones, so every atom keeps its AMBER-DYES name and type and the
pipeline's name-based machinery (C99/N99 attachment, the CA→dye observable,
the dihedral walk) works unchanged. Only the three thiosuccinimide linkers
have an open form; `L1R`/`L2R` are lysine amides and `B1R` an oxime, with no
ring to open.

| closed (kept, unchanged) | `O` — acid on the CH₂ side (N3–C9 broken) | `Q` — acid on the CH(S) side (N3–C10 broken) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `C1R` | `C1O` | `C1Q` |
| `C2R` | `C2O` | `C2Q` |
| `C3R` | `C3O` | `C3Q` |

Each open residue carries −1, gains `O5` (the carboxylate oxygen) and `HN3`
(the new amide hydrogen), and is fitted once with AM1-BCC on the acetyl-capped
ACE–X–NME residue so tleap can build it; the whole-label fit refits everything
per dye, so those charges only ever set the geometry. Provenance in
`param/open_linkers.json`; the residues are **added beside** the closed ones
via `newdyes.leaprc`, never in place of them, so a label can now be simulated
in either linkage.

**Two torsions had to be supplied.** The closed ring has no N–H, so AMBER-DYES
never needed a torsion about the amide that opening creates: `h7-ng-cg-c7`
(the `O` isomer) and `h7-ng-cg-c8` (the `Q` isomer) are missing. Every
`X-ng-cg-Y` amide torsion in `amberdyes.dat` carries the identical barrier
(2.500 kcal/mol, 180°, n=2), including `cg-ng-cg-c7`, `cG-ng-cg-c7` and
`cg-ng-cg-c8` — so both are **transferred by analogy** in
`param/open_linkers.frcmod`, which says so in its header. A parameter whose
origin is not stated cannot be defended.

**Alexa 488 first, per the priority set today.** All six `A48_C1O/C1Q/C2O/C2Q/
C3O/C3Q` labels build with `Errors = 0` at charge −3 (the dye's −2 plus the
linker's −1) and all six passed a CPU smoke test (297–301 K, stable energy).
The smoke was 50 ps and yields one frame — enough to prove the build and the
parameters, which is what it is for, not an ensemble.

**Queue order, as set by the user:** repairs (9 × 400 ns) → labels that lack
little (`BDF_B1R`, `BDF_L1R`, `T65_B1R`, 6 × 140 ns — each at 1/3 replicas,
where two runs turn a provisional score into a real one) → open ring (18 ×

## 2026-09-03d — the repair works, and the audit that judged it was wrong

**400 ns fixes AF4_C2R.** Scored on its own, the repair converges:

| set | pairwise overlaps | min | replica means (Å) | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 × 140 ns | 0.533, 0.779, 0.754 | **0.533** | 7.62 – 10.37 (spread 2.75) | FAIL |
| 3 × 400 ns | 0.904, 0.838, 0.910 | **0.838** | 7.70 – 8.72 (spread 1.02) | **PASS** |

The three 140 ns replicas sat in different basins — a 2.75 Å spread in mean
r(CA→dye) — and 400 ns lets each cross the barrier, closing the spread to
1.02 Å. Longer runs, not more of them, was the right prescription, and the six
queued Cy3B repairs (C3B_C2R 0.694, C3B_B1R 0.781) should proceed on the same
reasoning.

**But `audit_convergence.py` reported 0.533 after the repair, exactly as
before.** It pooled all six replicas, and between-replica agreement is a
minimum over pairs, so three good runs added to three bad ones cannot raise
it. The audit was reporting the failure the repair had just fixed, and left to
itself it would have marked the label FAIL in its library forever — and would
have made the six Cy3B runs look futile before they started.

Fixed: the audit now groups replicas **by their own recorded production
length** and scores each group separately; the verdict comes from the longest
length that has replicas to compare, with the others kept in the record
(`scored_on_ns`, `scored_on_runs`, `other_lengths`). AF4_C2R now reads
`PASS 0.838 3 rep @ 400 ns (also 140.0ns:0.533)` — both facts, neither hidden.

This is the same class of error as the hard-coded citations: a number that
looks like a measurement but answers a different question than the one asked.
The lesson generalises — **never compare runs of different lengths in a
statistic whose value is set by the worst pair.**

Standing: **7 pass, 3 provisional, 2 fail** (the two Cy3B labels in repair).

## 2026-09-07 — a stated limitation: the gate measures distance, not torsions

A parallel session (PRD-136) analysing this corpus reports that **single
140 ns free-dye runs are not converged in their torsion marginals** — slowest
implied timescales 5–190 ns, with `AF4_C2R_r5` showing 191 ns *at 400 ns* and
`BDF_L1R_r2` 124 ns — while dipole rotation is converged and replica-tight.

That is a stricter test than this project's gate, and it must be written down
rather than argued with. `audit_convergence.py` measures the overlap of
r(CA→dye) between independent replicas: a distance observable. The clustering
that produces a rotamer library works on **torsions**, and the cluster
populations depend on those marginals being sampled. So a label can pass the
gate at 0.80+ and still carry under-converged torsion populations — the
FRETpredict-protocol libraries inherit this, as do ours.

What this does and does not change:

* It does **not** invalidate the repairs. 400 ns demonstrably improved both
  the between-replica overlap *and* the halves test on all three repaired
  labels, so the longer runs are better sampled by every measure taken.
* It **does** mean the gate is necessary and not sufficient. A future audit
  should add a torsion-marginal convergence measure (implied timescale, or
  between-replica overlap of the dihedral histograms) beside the distance one.
* Per-label implied timescales, if PRD-136 shares them, belong in each
  library's provenance so a reader sees the limitation without having to know
  this note exists.

### 2026-09-07b — GPU sharing with PRD-136, and both handovers made automatic

Two campaigns now share heinzehub's two cards: this one (PRD-116, rotamer
libraries) and the dye-timewarp site campaign (PRD-136). My lanes started a
new job the instant one ended, so their queue — which waits for "no
`make_library.py` holding the card" — would have waited **forever**. That is
starvation, and my queue caused it.

Arrangement, decided by the owner (relayed through the PRD-136 session) after
both sessions independently recommended the same option:

1. `A48_B1R` × 3 runs first (~15 GPU-h). Promoted out of tier 4, where it sat
   at one replica; their site MD uses AMBER-DYES A48 parameters, so their seed
   corpus needs A48_B1R and **not** our GAFF2 `AF4_B1R` — the two must not be
   mixed in one seed set.
2. GPU 0 then goes to their campaign (~2–3 weeks); tier 4 continues on GPU 1
   alone, roughly doubling its remaining time.
3. GPU 0 comes back here when their campaign is done.

**Both handovers are automatic**, which matters more than the split itself:

* `run_queue_priority4.sh` checks, before claiming any job, whether it is on
  `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0` and whether all three `A48_B1R` logs carry the
  completion line; when both hold it prints "releasing GPU 0" and exits. The
  card frees itself after the job in flight ends — no kill, no race.
* `reclaim_gpu0.sh` waits for their `~/dtw/GPU0_RELEASED` marker **or** thirty
  minutes with no `s06_build_site.py` running, then waits for the card to be
  genuinely empty before resuming tier 4 on it. Marker *or* sustained absence,
  because a marker can be forgotten and a process check can race a restart.

Neither handover depends on anyone being awake. That is the whole point: an
arrangement that needs a human to notice a completion line is an arrangement
that silently stalls overnight.

Their queue also runs `A64_C2R` first — a label where we have a shipped
FRETpredict library but no trajectory — and appends `A64_C2R_r1`/`_r2` after
the site runs, so it reaches three replicas and can be scored by the gate
rather than sitting provisional. Cost to us: nothing.

Measured cost of sharing GPU 1 with their training: **~14 %** (681 ns/day
against 794 on the unshared card). Not worth moving anything for.

## 2026-09-07c — closing out: three objectives, then the GPUs go

The owner set three objectives and capped the campaign there: **finish the
labels that fail the gate, finish what is nearly done, complete the sets** —
then hand both cards to the dye-timewarp campaign (PRD-136). This supersedes
the sharing arrangement above: there is no GPU-0 handover and no reclaim,
because after the closing queue neither card comes back.

`run_queue_closing.sh` — 24 jobs, 5940 ns, ~4.2 days on two cards:

| objective | work | ns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. fix the failure | `T65_B1R` × 3 @ 400 ns (0.610; pairs 0.778/0.676/0.610) | 1230 |
| 2. finish in flight | `A48_C2O_r5`, `A48_C2Q_r3` (both ~80 % done) | — |
| 3a. complete the set | `A48` on `B1R`/`C2R`/`L1R`, × 3 @ 140 ns | 1350 |
| 3b. complete the set | `A48` ring-opened `C1Q`/`C3Q` @ 140, `C1O`/`C3O` @ 400 | 3360 |

**Dropped, not deferred:** the ~40-day tail — low-priority GAFF2 dyes and the
60 AMBER-DYES native combinations at one replica each. That is new coverage,
and the instruction was to close what is open.

Two decisions inside the plan worth keeping:

* **Run length follows the measurement, not a rule.** A label that failed at
  140 ns gets 400 ns (three confirmations). But the ring-opened **Q** isomer
  passes at 140 ns (`A48_C2Q` 0.904) while the **O** isomer does not
  (`A48_C2O` 0.758), so Q isomers run short and O isomers long. That
  asymmetry alone saves ~1.5 days over a blanket 400 ns.
* **`A48_C2Q` r4/r5 were cut and `A48` closed linkers added.** C2Q already
  passes on 3 × 140 ns, so a 400 ns trio buys nothing; meanwhile `A48` — the
  AMBER-DYES Alexa 488, the reference dye of the programme — had **no
  trajectory of ours on any closed linker**, only shipped libraries. That was
  the largest real gap and it is now filled.

`A48_C2Q_r3` is left to finish only because it is 80 % done; it is recorded as
a single 400 ns run and **not scored** — a lone long run cannot carry a
between-replica number, and mixing it with the 140 ns trio is exactly the
error the length-separated audit exists to prevent.

### 2026-09-07d — the ring-opened linker converges at 400 ns, and the Q/O split holds

`A48_C2O`, the first ring-opened label to get a 400 ns trio:

| set | pairwise overlaps | min | replica means (Å) | halves | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 × 140 ns | 0.887, 0.758, 0.851 | **0.758** | 10.57, 10.06, 9.07 | 0.824 | FAIL |
| 3 × 400 ns | 0.915, 0.887, 0.948 | **0.887** | 10.56, 9.99, 9.90 | 0.967 | **PASS** |

Fourth confirmation that 400 ns fixes a label that fails at 140, and the first
on chemistry AMBER-DYES does not ship. The replica-mean spread closes from
1.50 Å to 0.66 Å and the halves test rises 0.824 → 0.967.

**The Q/O split is now evidence, not a guess.** On the same linker skeleton and
the same dye, at 140 ns:

* `A48_C2Q` (acid on the CH–S side) — **0.904 PASS**
* `A48_C2O` (acid on the CH₂ side) — **0.758 FAIL**, needs 400 ns

Two regioisomers of one hydrolysis product, differing only in which carbonyl
opened, and one samples in a third of the time the other needs. That is the
basis for running the remaining Q isomers at 140 ns and the O isomers at
400 ns, which is what the closing queue does and what saves ~1.5 days.

Standing across audited labels: **12 pass, 1 fail** (`T65_B1R` 0.610, queued
for the same 400 ns repair).

### 2026-09-08 — the closing queue holds for a clean card, because waiting is faster

GPU 1 contention got worse: `A48_C1R` measured **216 ns/day** against 584
earlier the same evening and ~700 unshared. Not the label — `A48_C1R` is 11989
solvated atoms against `A48_C2Q_r3`'s 12178, same card, same platform. The
PRD-136 free-dye trainer (14.5 GB) had taken the card.

Their two lanes are on the critical path for a 09-14 conference, so they
declined to move the trainer. **The arithmetic says wait anyway:**

| `T65_B1R` × 3 (1230 ns), the last failing label | time | done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pushed through contention at 216 ns/day | 5.7 d | 09-15 |
| waiting for a clean card on 09-11, ~700 ns/day | 1.8 d | **09-12** |

**Three days sooner by not running.** So the hold is not a concession, it is
the faster plan; it would have been chosen on the numbers alone. Worth keeping
as a general point: on a contended card, waiting can dominate running, and the
only way to know is to measure the contended rate rather than assume a
fraction of the clean one.

Arrangement (`hold_after_c1r.sh`, detached):

* `A48_C1R` × 3 runs regardless — the reference label the protocol's parity bar
  was measured on, with no replica of ours, and the noise floor for their
  figure. ~2.1 days at the degraded rate.
* Everything after it stops until their "GPU 1 released" marker, or their
  trainer being gone from GPU 1 for thirty minutes. Then the queue resumes on
  its own with `T65_B1R`.
* GPU 0 stays theirs until `GPU0_RELEASED`.

Declined: running two of my replicas in parallel on the contended card. Two
jobs where a 14 GB trainer already dominates would most likely split the same
share rather than take more, and it makes both finish times unpredictable —
worse than slow.

Projected on a clean card from 09-11: objectives 1 and 3a by **09-14**; adding
the ring-opened set 3b pushes it to 09-19, which is the standing argument for
cutting it.
