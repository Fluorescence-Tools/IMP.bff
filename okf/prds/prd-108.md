# PRD-108: rotamer ensembles for FRET docking — R1 positions, the AV↔rotamer table, Langevin/Brownian dye samplers

Depends on [PRD-107](prd-107.md). Decisions (user, 2026-08-17 grilling):

1. **Docking integration is Python-only now.** A C++ `R1` position type
   inside `AVNetworkRestraint` (point cloud in the site frame, clash
   re-weighting via the PRD-105 occupancy map) is a follow-on gated by the
   AV↔rotamer table (stage 2).
2. **`simulation_type = "R1"`** = FRETpredict-style 1:1: the full library
   transformed into the site frame and screened against the protein, per
   rotamer **centre + dipole + weight** kept (all atoms retained), so the pair
   matrix R_ij, κ²_ij, w_i·w_j → FRET rate distribution and E_static/E_dyn are
   available downstream. R2/R3 reserved (R2 = sampled library, R3 = mixture).
   C++ warns on unknown types; `fps_positions_for_docking()` filters.
3. Object: **`RotamerEnsemble(fret.av.AccessibleVolume)`** — `points` (N,4) =
   centre + weight so every AV helper works unchanged; pair/rate kernel and κ²
   live in `IMP.bff.fret`.
4. AV↔rotamer differences are **recorded** (table authoritative), pins guard
   drift; not a gate.
5. Samplers: real Langevin, `integrator="md"|"bd"`; the collision walk stays
   `sample-dof-walk`; thermodynamic pins (equilibrium populations vs
   Boltzmann `generate_linker_rotamers`, md ⇄ bd, oscillator temperature,
   free-particle MSD).

### Stage 2 — AV↔rotamer cross-validation table (hGBP1 481, T4L) (done 2026-08-17)
* `cgdye/rotamer/compare_av.py` (`compare_positions`, `compare_pairs`,
  `ensemble_pair_statistics`, `markdown_table`, `summary_numbers`; exports
  `compare_av_and_rotamer_positions/pairs`), script
  `scripts/compare_av_rotamer.py` = `dye rotamer compare-av` (the `rotamer`
  group is now attached to `dye`; `cli.main` defined — it was called but never
  existed). Writes **`okf/validation/av_vs_rotamer.md`** (authoritative) and
  `test/references/cgdye_av_vs_rotamer_pins.json`;
  `test/cgdye/rotamer/medium_test_av_vs_rotamer.py` pins the numbers exactly
  and asserts loose sanity bounds only (Δmean < 20 Å, Δ⟨R_DA⟩ < 20 Å, no
  weighted rotamer inside the protein).
* **What the table says (2026-08-17, T = 298.15 K, no DH, R0 = 52 Å,
  Alexa488 C1R donors / Alexa594 C1R acceptors, AV positions as authored with
  the default FPS strip):**
  - Position clouds: AV rms extent 11–13 Å vs rotamer 8–12 Å (cutoff10) /
    5–10 Å (cutoff30); Δ mean position median 5–7 Å, max 13–14 Å.
  - Pairs: |Δ⟨R_DA⟩| median 3–4 Å (T4L), 6–12 Å (hGBP1 481 pairs), max ≈ 10–16 Å.
  - **T4L against the 99 experimental distances: Σχ² AV 155, rotamer 257
    (cutoff10) / 141 vs AV 52 on the 66 pairs where the cutoff-30 ensembles are
    valid** — the AV model fits the T4L data clearly better than the screened
    libraries. With cutoff30, 33 of 99 T4L pairs involve a site where every
    rotamer clashes (Z < 0.05, e.g. 22D: FRETpredict's uniform fallback,
    flagged ‡).
  - FRETpredict's soft screening (σ-scaling 0.5) leaves 65–85 % of the
    ensemble weight on rotamers whose heavy atoms come within 2.5 Å of the
    protein (min 1.3–1.6 Å for w > 1e-3) — recorded per position.
* **Gate decision for the C++ `R1` follow-on:** not motivated by this table —
  rotamer ensembles neither reproduce the AVs nor fit the T4L data better;
  the useful follow-on is per-dye *AV-parameter calibration* against the
  ensembles (linker length/width from the rotamer extents) and a harder
  screening (σ-scaling 1) study, both recorded as follow-ons.
### Stage 3 — Langevin / Brownian dye samplers with thermodynamic validation (done 2026-08-17)
* `cgdye/sampling/langevin.py`: `LangevinDyeSampler(protein_hier, dye_hier,
  dye_mol2, chain, residue, *, integrator="md"|"bd", temperature, timestep_fs,
  friction_ps, interaction_sphere, repulsion_k, seed)` — the dye force field
  (`topology/combined.dye_forcefield_system`: bonds/angles/dihedrals + LJ
  lower bounds; anchor `N CA C O` fixed on the residue) plus a soft-sphere
  repulsion against the protein heavy atoms around the site
  (`CloseBipartitePairContainer` + `SoftSpherePairScore`), integrated by
  `IMP.atom.MolecularDynamics` + `LangevinThermostatOptimizerState` (md,
  2 fs) or `IMP.atom.BrownianDynamics` with Stokes–Einstein `Diffusion` (bd,
  0.5 fs — with 2000 kcal/mol/Å² bonds overdamped BD is stable only for
  dt < k_BT/(Dk) ≈ 2 fs); `make_simulator` is the reusable driver;
  `.run()` → `LangevinTrajectory`. `dye sample-langevin --integrator md|bd`,
  `scripts/langevin_hgbp1_site481.py`; exports `IMP.bff.LangevinDyeSampler`,
  `make_langevin_simulator`, `dye_forcefield_system`, `torsion_cosine`.
* **Thermodynamic pins.** `test/cgdye/test_langevin_sampler.py`: harmonic
  oscillator ⟨V⟩ = 3/2 k_BT and kinetic T (md, ±10 %); free Brownian particle
  MSD = 6Dt (±15 %); the attached dye at hGBP1 481 (anchor never moves, dye
  moves, T_kin ≈ 300 K, no mobile heavy atom inside the protein).
  `medium_test_langevin_torsion.py`: a 3-fold cosine torsion sampled by md
  and bd follows exp(−V/kT) (bin-averaged valley/peak ratio ±25 %, three
  wells; interwell exchange is slow — residence ~100s of ps — so with a few
  ns the well populations still scatter by ~0.1) and md ⇄ bd agree.
  `expensive_test_langevin_equilibrium.py`: md (1.2 ns) and bd (0.4 ns) agree
  on the dye-centroid distance distribution at hGBP1 481 (~7 min).
* **Physics bug found and fixed on the way:** cgdye's torsion types are in
  the CHARMM convention V = k(1 + cos(nφ − δ)) (`T_PI`: n=2, δ=π planar;
  `T_LINK`: n=3, δ=0 staggered) but were handed to `IMP.core.Cosine`, which
  scores k(1 − cos(nφ − δ')) — so every conjugated torsion had its minimum at
  90° (score 24 kcal/mol at planar!) and every linker torsion sat eclipsed.
  `topology/dye.py::torsion_cosine` does the δ' = δ + π conversion; used by
  `sim/dye_restraints.py` and `sim/runner.py` (the MD runner had the same
  defect). `test_dye_topology.py::TestTorsionConvention`.
* **Cost / what it is good for.** md: ~2000 steps/s for the 79-atom dye
  against 464 obstacles (≈ 4 ns/hour on this laptop); bd 4× slower per ps.
  Over 0.6 ns the dye's atoms fluctuate by ~0.9 Å rms and displace up to
  5 Å — the linker's conformational space (AV extent ~12 Å) needs ns–µs, so
  the sampler is the physical dynamics tool, not a fast cloud generator (the
  Metropolis library sampler and the rotamer libraries are). The plan's
  "Langevin populations vs `generate_linker_rotamers` clusters" comparison
  was not done: the two use different energy functions (repulsive-only
  internal LJ vs the full bonded FF), so it would not be a consistency test.
* Example `examples/structure/hgbp1_explicit_dye_fret.py` (ensembles → FRET
  → fps.json R1 → Langevin), manual notebook section 6 updated.
* `distances_from_ensembles` writes ⟨R_DA⟩_E with κ² = 2/3 (the fps.json /
  AVNetworkRestraint convention; ``kappa2="dipoles"`` for the orientation-
  resolved value).
