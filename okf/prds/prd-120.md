# PRD-120: the Labelizer, natively — a 1:1 port, A/B'd, and one container out

**Status:** done (2026-08-24)
**Done:** The Labelizer ported 1:1: per-residue label-site score, FRET pair score, the PTO.MFDB vocabulary layer, and `imp_bff_labelizer`. A/B against the reference in `okf/validation/labelizer_ab.md`; a written container passes `pto_ebml_check`.
**Missing:** Nothing.
**Next:** Nothing -- closed.


## Why

`../labelizer-backend/src_labelizer/labelizer/` is the reference implementation
of the published label-site score (Gebhardt *et al.*, *Nat. Commun.* **16**,
3305, 2025) — 17 modules, ~4.2 kLOC of Python. It answers the first question
anyone asks before a FRET experiment: *where do I put the dye, and which pair
do I measure?* `IMP.bff` could answer neither, because it has no per-residue
scoring layer at all — nothing in `include/` iterated residues and emitted a
score column.

Three things made porting the right move rather than depending on it:

1. **It does not run.** `ss` shells out to DSSP and raises
   `RuntimeError("Unknown platform")` on macOS outright
   (`secondary_structure.py:126`); `se` and `me` shell out to MSMS, whose
   bundled binaries are 32-bit ppc/i386 Mach-O; and it pins
   `labellib==2020.10.5`, **banned here** by owner rule (2026-08-11).
2. **The physics is already ours.** AVs, R₀, κ², PET quenchers, Shrake–Rupley
   SASA, charge masks, FASPR. What the reference adds on top is a scoring
   layer, and that is all this PRD is.
3. **The vocabulary was already waiting.** `mmfdb_flr_ext.dic` carries a full
   `_mmfdb_label_score` category whose `score_type` enumeration *is*
   Labelizer's seven parameters, and whose description names its two-letter
   tags explicitly. Nothing wrote it.

**Scope, per the owner: the original faithfully, then measure.** Not the
improved model. `prototypes/fast_label_score/` is the improved one and is
untouched — the original has to land on a codebase that runs before there is
anything to measure an improvement against.

## What the A/B found

Four terms have reference output. Two reproduce **exactly**, one is exact on an
input that is itself broken, and one is characterised:

* **`ss` 195/195 exact.** Two real corrections got there: DSSP marks the
  residues *between* an n-turn's H-bonded pair (not the donor), and **DSSP 3.0
  reversed the π/α precedence** (Touw 2015) — the reference's CSVs were made
  with the later behaviour.
* **`cr` 195/195 exact.**
* **`cs` exact to 16 digits, on an unreproducible reference.** The shipped
  example passes its conservation PDB as *both* input and output, so each run
  feeds the previous run's scores back in as grades. The lookup has a
  two-cycle, so the shipped CSV holds **two distinct values across 195
  residues** where the table has ten bins. Our lookup reproduces both to the
  last digit; the input that produced them is one generation back and no longer
  exists in the distribution.
* **`se` 74.9 % bin-exact, no bias.** Native surface vs MSMS: mean +0.015 Å,
  r = 0.976, 92.3 % within one table bin. The residual is analytic-vs-sampled
  scatter at the quantisation floor, not a correctable offset.

**A second structure closed the biggest gap.** 1DDB is all-helical, so the
strand half of DSSP was unexercised. The paper's Supplementary Data 1 also
carries maltose-binding protein in **two conformations** (1OMP apo, 1ANF holo,
370 residues, 72 `E` and 5 `B` in the reference's own column). Against it:
`cr` **370/370**, `ss` **97.3 % on both conformations** — and getting there
found two defects invisible on an all-helical protein. **β-bulges** were
fragmenting sheets into isolated bridges (ladders were joined only between
strictly adjacent bridges; now a union-find joins any two of a type that
advance together with one side stepping exactly one), and **`G` was emitted as
a leftover stub** after `H` had taken part of its span (a 3-10 helix is three
residues or it is not one). The ten interior strand residues that remain are
not an energy-threshold artifact: −0.5 kcal/mol is optimal (94.3 % at −0.4,
94.9 % at −0.6), so what is left is DSSP's sheet-level bookkeeping.

The two conformations also gave the **pair layer** its first real exercise. It
ranks MBP's hinge closure first — the top twenty two-state pairs all move by
more than 5 Å and eighteen contract, by 11–13 Å — and A29, in the best pair, is
a site the paper itself labelled.

And one thing worth knowing for anyone using the pair layer: **the analytic
cone systematically over-reaches by 2–5 Å**, and because the pair score peaks
sharply at `R = R₀` that is a ~30 % score error. The top five pairs by cone
score are *not* the top five after AV refinement. `n_refine` should not be left
at zero for real work.

## Decisions worth keeping

**The published arithmetic is the default, defects included** (`LlModel`).
Three defects change a number and all three are reproduced, because the paper's
values were computed with them: the joined label score uses `prod ** 0.5` where
the geometric mean is `prod ** (1/N)` (`fret_score.py:220`, correct line
commented out beneath it); a **weight-zero** term that reads exactly `0.0` still
zeroes the whole score (`labeling_score.py:166`); and the configurable excluded
amino acid is written to `bad_aa` and read from `self.aa`, so it is always
methionine (`labelizer.py:233`). `LL_MODEL_CORRECTED` fixes all three and the
container records which was used.

**Absence replaced the sentinel.** The reference writes `-1` for "excluded" and
`0` for "no contribution" into the same column as real scores, so its CSVs
cannot be read back. A position with no score now carries a `status` and **no
value** — which is what `_mmfdb_label_score.value` demands in as many words.

**Dictionary additions, upstream first.** `_mmfdb_artifact.row_grain` had
`pair` but nothing for a position on a polymer, and `mfdb_check_term` refused
to write one — which is the term layer working. `label_site` went into
`../mmfdb/src/mmfdb/data/mmfdb_flr_ext.dic`, `_dictionary.version` **1.7 →
1.8** (uncommitted there; that repo had other work in flight).

Seven attribution items followed, at the `.drot` session's request, because a
converted rotamer library is somebody else's measurement in this package's
format and its licence has to travel with it: `author`, `citation`, `license`,
`terms`, `terms_url`, `source`, `redistributed_via` on `_mmfdb_artifact`, with
`MfdbAttribution` / `mfdb_attribution_tags` over them. Three decisions in
those, all theirs and all right: attribution is **per artifact**, since one
family container can hold GPL-3.0-only and academic-use libraries at once;
**`license` is not mandatory-SPDX** — "free for academic use" has no identifier
and coining a `LicenseRef` would state something the upstream did not, so at
least one of `license`/`terms` is required and neither is preferred; and
`redistributed_via` earns a field of its own because Dunbrack-2010 arrives via
FASPR, which is MIT while the library is not. Spelled `license`, US, to match
the eighteen occurrences in the upstream mmCIF dictionaries.

**The vocabularies are compiled in, not parsed at run time.** `../mmfdb` is not
a dependency and writing a file must not require a checkout. Same choice
`ProbeLibrary.h` and `FPSSchema.h` already made, with the same obligation:
`test/label/test_pto_profile.py` re-reads both dictionaries and fails on drift.
A term is checked, never recalled.

**The container is `imp-bff-69`'s, the vocabulary is ours.** Agreed with that
session: they own `drot.*` and `rot.bbdep.*`, this owns `label.*`; they do no
term validation and asked for the layer to be reusable rather than
labelizer-scoped, which is why it is `PtoProfile.h`. Tags are additive and
never a decoding dependency — a walker plus the framing reads a container with
none of this code.

## Two kernels extended, not copied

* `SolventAccessibleSurface.h` gained
  `solvent_accessible_surface_area_per_atom`. The existing kernel samples every
  atom at one radius and scales by `vdw²`, which is right for contact estimates
  and wrong for a *relative* accessibility. Both now exist and the header says
  which question each answers. It also gained its first consumer: the original
  was written for PRD-109 and nothing had ever called it.
* Sites are placed **once** in the pair layer. The reference rebuilds the inner
  site's AV inside the inner loop (`fret_score.py:653`), making its exact mode
  quadratic in AV builds. Numerically neutral, so not behind `LlModel`.

## What IMP's modelling could buy, and what it would cost

Asked during the port: this now runs on IMP, which can close loops and model
structures — does that help? Checked rather than assumed. `IMP.kinematics` is
installed and has **`CCDLoopClosure.h`** (cyclic coordinate descent);
`IMP.modeller` is **not** installed. FASPR side-chain packing is already in
this package (`Faspr.h`, PRD-118). Three places it would genuinely help, none
of which belong in a 1:1 port:

1. **Build the cysteine mutant before measuring the volume.** The score asks
   "how good would a *cysteine* be here", and the accessible volume is
   currently computed against the wild-type side chain with the fps strip
   applied. FASPR could build the actual mutant. This is the most defensible of
   the three and needs no new dependency — `Faspr.h` and `StripMask.h` are both
   here.
2. **Unresolved loops.** A missing loop currently means those sites are not
   scored at all, and (since the sequence-gap fix) that `ll_dssp` correctly
   refuses to bridge the gap rather than inventing a turn across it. CCD could
   build the loop and make those sites scorable.
3. **Unnatural amino acid sites.** The pAcF series is a large share of the
   best per-site photophysics in `prototypes/fast_label_score/okf`, and a UAA
   is dropped by the residue filter today.

The cost is the same in all three, and it is not small: **the likelihood tables
were fitted on unmodelled structures.** A modelled loop's depth and exposure
are predictions, and feeding them to a table fitted on crystallographic
observations produces a score that is not on the same scale as the paper's —
reported, if nothing marks it, with exactly the same confidence as a resolved
site. Any of this therefore needs a per-position provenance flag saying the
coordinates were modelled, which is a schema question before it is a modelling
question (`_mmfdb_label_score.status` has `unresolved`; it has no term for
"scored on modelled coordinates").

So: worth doing, worth doing after the A/B rather than inside it, and (1) first
because it is cheapest and changes the least.

## The fluorophore database: where it should live

Asked as part of "can imp.bff replace the labelizer backend": the dye database
must be MMFDB- and flrCIF-conformant, and chisurf must be looked at. It was,
and the answer changed the plan.

**ChiSurf already hosts the database, and it is already the MMFDB schema.**
`chisurf/plugins/spectra_downloader/spectra.db` holds **2 288 probes, 19 882
optical properties and 2 896 spectra** in tables named `probes`,
`optical_properties` and `spectra`, with flrCIF column names
(`chromophore_name`, `probe_origin`, `probe_link_type`) and the full `flr_*`
and `mmfdb_*` families beside them. imp.bff's `probe_library.cif` (40 probes) and
Labelizer's `resources/fluorophores/` (15) are small bundled subsets of the
same upstream data — every one of the 13 shared dyes agrees on quantum yield
and extinction coefficient exactly.

So the storage question was already answered. **What was missing is the
vocabulary**, and that gap was real: no dictionary in the stack declared an
item for a quantum yield, an extinction coefficient or a fluorescence
spectrum. The only near matches are `_em_detector.detective_quantum_efficiency`
(a detector), `_refine.ls_extinction_*` (crystallographic absorption) and
`_pdbx_nmr_spectral_*` (a different measurement). `probe_library.cif`'s own
header had recorded this and asked for the items to be proposed.

Added to `mmfdb_flr_ext.dic` in the 1.8 pass, describing the tables that
already exist rather than a parallel model: **`_mmfdb_optical_property.*`**
(id, probe_id, property_name, property_value, unit) and **`_mmfdb_spectrum.*`**
(id, probe_id, spectrum_type, wavelengths, intensity_values, wavelength_unit,
intensity_unit).

Three things the stored data forced, and they are why this had to be checked
rather than designed:

* **`spectrum_type` is not only dye types.** The table is shared with the
  optical components a measurement is made through — 850 `transmission`, 69
  `quantum_efficiency`, plus `reflectance` and `responsivity`. Declaring only
  absorption/excitation/emission would have made every stored filter row
  non-conformant.
* **`wavelength_unit` is `nm`, not `nanometres`.** The `_mmfdb_column.units`
  vocabulary says `nanometres`, and all 2 896 stored spectra say `nm`. The
  dictionary follows the data; renaming would invalidate every row for nothing.
* **`property_name` is uncontrolled today and the enumeration says so.** Those
  19 882 rows carry **76 distinct names** written by importers that never
  agreed — `qy` beside `q_fluor`, `ext_coeff` beside `molar_extinction`,
  `em_max` beside `Emission max`, some mojibake — and `unit` is NULL on every
  one. The enumeration is the canonical list; the item description states
  plainly that it is not an inventory of what is stored, so a reader does not
  assume a missing unit means dimensionless.

**Name resolution, in imp.bff.** The library keys are vendor spellings and
nobody types them: all fifteen Labelizer dye names failed an exact lookup while
the right rows sat in the library. `resolve_probe_name` folds case and
punctuation and expands the two known abbreviations (`Alexa` → `AlexaFluor`,
`Cy` → `LumiprobeCy`), requires a unique hit, and raises on ambiguity rather
than picking — `Cy5` resolves because `LumiprobeCy55` is a different fold.
All 15 now resolve. Thirteen were already present under vendor spellings with
QY and extinction coefficient matching exactly — both sets descend from the
same upstream data. **`Atto532` and `Atto643` were genuinely absent** from
imp.bff's library *and* from chisurf's, and are now imported from Labelizer's
own tables by `utility/import_labelizer_dyes.py` (40 dyes, up from 38).

The import is small but has three ways to go quietly wrong, so each is checked
rather than assumed. The spectra are resampled onto this library's 300–970 nm
grid, because `spectral_overlap` throws unless every dye shares one and
Labelizer's tables stop at 821 and 900 nm (zero-padded beyond, as the reference
does). Both curves are peak-normalised, which is not cosmetic: the donor's
emission is divided by its own integral so its scale cancels, but the
acceptor's excitation is multiplied by the extinction coefficient to give a
molar absorptivity. And the peaks are pinned — ATTO532 and ATTO643 are named
for their excitation maxima, so a grid offset or a swapped excitation/emission
pair, both of which still load and still give a plausible-looking R0, move them
and fail. Measured: 532/552 nm and 644/665 nm against the vendor's 532/553 and
643/662, and R0(ATTO532→ATTO647N) = 58.2 Å against ATTO-TEC's ~59 Å.

A dye that really is absent still fails loudly: R0 goes as the sixth root of
the overlap, so a substituted spectrum is a quietly wrong distance.

**Not done, and it is a migration rather than an edit**: mapping those 76
`property_name` values onto the canonical eight, and backfilling `unit`. That
touches 19 882 rows in another repo's database and needs its owner.

## The spectra as a container

`data/dyes.mmfdb.pto` — the bundled dye library written through the vocabulary
above, so a labelling run can take its \(R_0\) from a file whose every name
resolves to a dictionary item. `ProbeContainer.h`: `probe_write_pto`,
`probe_read_pto`, `probe_library_to_pto`, `probe_pto_forster_radius`.

Two tabular objects, because they count different things and the profile
forbids joining by position:

| object | kind | grain | rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| `probes.json` | `mfdb.probes` | `species` | one per dye: flrCIF identity, plus scalars as `_mmfdb_optical_property` name/value/unit triples |
| `spectra.json` | `mfdb.spectra` | `curve_point` | one per (dye, spectrum_type, wavelength) point |

joined by a declared edge (`maps_rows_of`, `_flr_probe_list.chromophore_name`
→ `_mmfdb_spectrum.probe_id`). Kinds are `mfdb.` rather than `label.`, since
nothing in it is specific to label-site scoring. Both grains already existed;
no new terms were needed.

Verified: all 40 probes round-trip with every scalar and spectrum identical, and
\(R_0\) through the container equals \(R_0\) from the CIF to 1e-9 — the
container costs no accuracy, which matters because \(R_0\) goes as the sixth
root of the overlap.

**A NaN stays absent.** The library spells "not carried" as NaN and the
container omits the property rather than writing 0, because the bundled tables
contain genuine zero quantum yields and the two must not become the same row.

### The unit, which nearly went wrong, and then stopped being a hazard

**Current state (2026-08-25): the package is Ångström throughout.**
`forster_radius`, `probe_pto_forster_radius` and every consumer of an \(R_0\) —
`LlFretOptions` (default 52), `av_distance`, `fret_efficiency` — are all Å.
Spectra remain nanometres, which is what a wavelength is. There is no
conversion left at any call site.

It arrived that way through a near-miss worth keeping, because the same shape
will recur wherever two units meet. `forster_radius` used to return
**nanometres** (prefactor 0.02108) while everything consuming its result was
Å. Wiring the derived radius into `imp_bff_labelizer` without converting would
have scored every pair against an \(R_0\) ten times too small — which does not
read as an error, it reads as a protein where no pair is worth measuring. The
first fix was a single `× 10` inside `probe_pto_forster_radius`, documented as
the one place the two met.

That was the weaker answer, and the session porting `%pythoncode` to C++
argued the stronger one: **one unit, no conversions.** They audited every
caller of the nm-returning functions, found six `× 10` sites and that nothing
actually mixed them, and moved the conversion inside `forster_radius` itself.
Two of the six sites were this port's and are now plain calls.

**Finishing it (2026-08-26).** Auditing the migration on the owner's
instruction that *every* Förster radius be Å found two things it had left:

* `forster_radius_from_spectra` still matched names on whitespace alone, so
  `Alexa488` resolved through `find_probe` and failed through the name-based
  route — one spelling working through one door and not the other, and the
  failure reading as a missing dye rather than a missing alias. It now shares
  `resolve_probe_name`.
* `test/references/cgprobe_fretpredict_pins.json` (then `cgdye_…`) still stored `R0_nm`, with two
  tests multiplying or dividing by ten to meet it. The pin had a defensible
  reason — it is FRETpredict's published number and holding it in FRETpredict's
  unit is what makes it a citation — so the value moved to `R0_angstrom` and a
  sibling `R0_source` field records the published nm figure in words. The
  citation survives; the arithmetic leaves the assertions.

Pinned in `test/label/test_probe_container.py`: **all three producers**
(`forster_radius`, `forster_radius_from_spectra`, `probe_pto_forster_radius`)
agree to 1e-9 across five dye pairs and every spelling, and each value lands in
the 20–120 Å band — the assertion that catches a factor of ten reappearing at
any end.

### Names

`resolve_probe_name` (and the container's own lookup, sharing the same
`dye_fold_name`/`dye_name_matches` primitives rather than a second copy of the
rules) folds case and punctuation and expands the two vendor abbreviations.
Without it every one of the fifteen Labelizer dye names failed while the right
row sat in the library, because the keys are vendor spellings — `AlexaFluor488`,
`LumiprobeCy3`, `ATTO647N` — and nobody types those.

`imp_bff_labelizer --donor Alexa488 --acceptor Alexa647 [--dyes X.mmfdb.pto]`
derives the radius and prints where it came from, so a run says whether its
\(R_0\) was measured or asserted.

## What changed after it shipped

The port landed 2026-08-24; the package moved under it. Recorded here because
the PRD is the only place that says what the labelizer *is*, and a reader who
finds a symbol that no longer exists has no way to tell a rename from a
deletion.

### `DyeLibrary` became `ProbeLibrary` (2026-08-26)

The species type is `Probe`, not `Dye`, and carries a `ProbeType` of `dye` /
`fluorescent_protein` / `spin_label` / `unspecified`. `find_probe`,
`read_probe_library`, `available_probes`, `resolve_probe_name`. `DyeLibrary.h`
and `DyeContainer.h` are **deleted, not shimmed** — the owner's call for a
pre-release tree, and the right one: one name for the thing.

This is not cosmetic for the labelizer. \(R_0\) is the pair layer's whole
sensitivity axis, and `forster_radius` used to refuse a non-fluorescent probe
with *"both dyes need a spectrum"*, which reads as a missing file. It now names
the probe and the type. A spin label has no spectrum by construction; that is a
category error, not an absent input.

### The library CIF had a column that lied

`_bff_dye.probe_type` held `ATTO`, `Lumiprobe`, `AlexaFluor` — a **vendor**,
under the name of a type. Nothing noticed because the reader never read it. The
categories are now `_bff_probe` / `_bff_probe_spectrum`, the vendor column is
called `vendor` and is read into `Probe::vendor` rather than dropped, and
`probe_type` means what it says. All 40 rows are `dye`.

Worth stating as a defect class rather than an incident: it is the same shape
as the reference's `-1`/`0` sentinel collision that this port exists to fix — a
value that is wrong in a way nothing exercises. The fix in both cases is that
something reads it.

### The 1.8 dictionary pass grew

The PRD index still described 1.8 as one term (`row_grain = label_site`). It
carries, in the end: `row_grain` += `label_site` and `spectrum`; the categories
`_mmfdb_optical_property.*` and `_mmfdb_spectrum.*`; seven attribution items on
`_mmfdb_artifact` (`author`, `citation`, `license`, `terms`, `terms_url`,
`source`, `redistributed_via`); `_mmfdb_probe.{probe_id,probe_type,vendor}`;
and `clustering` on `_mmfdb_operation.operation_type`.

The last of those is not the labelizer's, and is recorded because the reason
generalises: the rotamer session needed to say **which protocol** built a
`.drot` library, `mfdb_operation_tags` throws on a non-term, and the closest
existing value (`analysis`) would have been a lie nothing checks.
`_mmfdb_probe` is in the mmfdb namespace rather than on `_flr_probe_list`
because that namespace belongs to IHM-FLR and a future item of the same name
would collide.

### Renames elsewhere the labelizer rode out

`cgdye` → `cgprobe`, `AVNetworkRestraint` → `ProbeNetworkRestraint`,
`test/references/cgdye_*.json` → `cgprobe_*.json`, and the deletion of
`DyeSampling.h`, `DyeDiffusion.h`, `DyeForceField.h`, `CifIO.h` and
`LabelingRestraints.h`. None of them reach the labelizer: it includes only
`AV*.h`, `StatesDistance.h`, `SolventAccessibleSurface.h`, `ProbeLibrary.h`,
`Pto.h` and its own headers. That the port survived a restructuring of this
size untouched is the strongest evidence that the reuse rule ("code like AV
must not be duplicated") was actually followed.

## Open

* **No β-rich reference case.** 1DDB is all-helical, so the DSSP bridge/ladder
  path is exercised (1anf, 3j0e) but never *compared*. This is the most
  valuable thing that could be added.
* The native surface is the **contact** surface only; MSMS includes the
  reentrant patches. That is where the remaining `se` scatter should live.
* `tp` and `ce` are ported and working but **unvalidated** — both crash on load
  in the reference and carry weight 0 in the published model.
* The two-conformation pair score and the Cβ difference map have no reference
  output at all.
* Whether `published` should stay the default, whether `tp`/`ce` earn a weight,
  and whether the pair layer should rank with `GreedyOlga` are the owner's
  calls now that there are numbers.
* **Non-standard residues are dropped by `ll_read_structure`**, so a UAA
  labelling site vanishes from the per-residue arrays. Scores are keyed by
  `(asym_id, seq_id)` so nothing downstream shifts, and `ll_dssp` no longer
  bridges the gap — but the site itself is unscorable. Raised by
  `fast-label-score-77`, whose bundle has 982 pAcF measurements across 75
  (protein, site) pairs. The fix is a filter that keeps polymer residues by
  backbone rather than by a name whitelist; it is not a 1:1 behaviour and so
  waits for the clean-up pass.
