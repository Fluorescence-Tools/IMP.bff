# PRD-139: the factor graph, enhanced to carry a Bayesian decay analysis

**Status**: written 2026-09-08, not started. **Next**: step 1.

## Why

`FactorGraph` was ported from ChiSurf to make a global fit's factorisation
explicit (PRD-68): which datasets a parameter touches, how the problem
decomposes into blocks and separators, what its treewidth is. It carries
variables (key, name, index, fit index) and factors of two kinds, PRIOR and
LIKELIHOOD.

The sibling project `../ucfret` has a Bayesian analysis of time-resolved
fluorescence decays whose posterior is exactly the object this class
describes -- 113 parameters, eight to twelve histograms, a distance
distribution shared by every histogram, per-histogram nuisances, a smoothing
hyperparameter -- and it cannot be represented faithfully today
(`../ucfret/okf/prd-laplace-reference-implementation.md`, step R3; the owner,
2026-09-08: "enhance the bff factor graph so that it can also handle the
bayesian decay processing"). Three things are missing, and each one changes
an answer the class already gives.

1. **A variable has no size.** The distance distribution is 25 spline
   coefficients, the donor lifetime spectrum 33, the rotational weights 9,
   a background 1. `block_cost` and every judgement built on it treat them
   alike, so the sampling blocks and their costs are wrong by two orders of
   magnitude for this model.
2. **A variable has no role.** Physics, calibration, instrument, per-channel
   nuisance and hyperparameter behave differently in every downstream use
   (what may be held, what is reported, what a block should contain), and
   the caller has to keep a parallel dictionary to know which is which.
3. **There is no hyperparameter factor.** The roughness prior of a decay
   analysis is not a prior on one variable: it couples every distribution
   coefficient AND the weight that scales it, and integrating that weight out
   is what makes the analysis work. Declared as a PRIOR it is
   indistinguishable from a Gaussian on a single constant, and the elimination
   order that follows is not the one the analysis uses.

A fourth thing is missing for use rather than for faithfulness: **a graph
cannot be saved or loaded**, so it cannot be stored beside the data it
describes or handed to another process.

## What

1. **Variable size and role.** `add_variable(key, name, index, fit_index,
   size = 1, role = "")`, both stored and returned by new accessors; `size`
   enters `block_cost` (the cost of a block is the product over its
   variables' sizes, not the count of them) and `describe()`. Existing calls
   keep working: the defaults are today's behaviour.
2. **A hyperparameter factor kind.** `HYPER = 2` beside `PRIOR` and
   `LIKELIHOOD`, with the same scope semantics; `get_factor_kind(key)` to read
   it back. Counted separately in `describe()`, and available to the
   elimination order as a factor that couples its whole scope.
3. **Serialisation.** `to_json()` / `from_json(str)` and `save(path)` /
   `load(path)` writing the variables (key, name, index, fit index, size,
   role) and the factors (key, kind, scope by variable key, fit index, size).
   Round trip is exact.
4. **Python tests** in `test/factorgraph/`: the decay model's graph built as
   `../ucfret` builds it (one distribution variable of size 25 in the scope of
   every likelihood factor, per-histogram scatter and background in exactly
   one each, a HYPER factor over the distribution and its weight); its
   components, treewidth, separators and sampling blocks asserted; the block
   costs asserted to follow the sizes; a save/load round trip asserted equal.

## Done when

- `add_variable` with a size and a role round-trips through
  `get_variable_size`/`get_variable_role`, and `block_cost` of a block
  containing the 25-coefficient variable is 25 times the cost of the same
  block with a scalar in its place (the check that can fail: today they are
  equal).
- A HYPER factor is counted in `describe()`, is returned by
  `get_factor_kind`, and appears in `factors_of` for every variable in its
  scope; a graph with the decay model's hyper factor has the treewidth the
  model has.
- `save`/`load` of the decay model's graph reproduces every field, and
  `describe()` of the loaded graph equals `describe()` of the original.
- The new Python test file passes, and the existing factor-graph tests are
  unchanged and still pass.
- `../ucfret` builds its model's graph through the new interface and reports
  bff's decomposition of it (that consumer step is PRD R3(a) there, not here).
