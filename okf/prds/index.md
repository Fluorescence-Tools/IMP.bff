## bff-local PRDs

* 🚧 [PRD-135: av.pinn — a general forward PINN that replaces the quenching solver, and works in reverse](prd-135.md) — **status: active (2026-09-06).** A voxel-encoder network `(structure, physical parameters) → VV(t), VH(t)` trained on a continuous stream of solver output over random PDB sites (natural-frequency decoys, Sobol sweeps, ~3,800 unique samples/h), emitting a calibrated covariance that reaches the reverse fit. Forward acceptance met on the frozen 300k checkpoint — τ_x **0.102 ns**, r_inf **0.0199**, z-sd 0.99, coverage 0.94 on 920 held-out geometries — with r_inf straddling its bar between checkpoints for a reason that was measured, not guessed: a loss floor set by the ill-conditioned quarter of sites, five interventions null. Reverse recovery passes at **88 %** coverage against solver data once the surrogate error fell below the measurement σ; the defect that had made every earlier reverse run read "31/31 identified, 0/31 covered" was a non-finite autograd Hessian, replaced by the Gauss-Newton information. The identifiability study answers the literature search: ~45 sites with both observables pin the quenching chemistry (`kq_TRP` 43), ~90 the rotational and sticking physics, the dynamic sticking scales never; lifetimes alone suffice for the quenching constants, anisotropies are needed for the rest. Against the 23 measured sites, per site and leave-one-out, τ_x ρ **+0.35** — indistinguishable from the descriptor line's +0.42 at this n — and the model reproduces the measured scale (through-origin slope 1.02 ± 0.03).

* 📐 [PRD-119: quenching anisotropy from the rotamers — a decision-gated plan (kill-test the continuum first, then an exchange-coupled rotamer master equation with learned terms)](prd-119.md) — **status: proposed, plan only.** Stage 0 puts the 17-parameter continuum twin on trial per site (surrogate reachability + quench/static floors): if ≥ ~85–90 % of the 41 sites are reachable and the global fit beats the parameter-free s30 baseline (`tau_x` ρ +0.571) by a real margin, the continuum is adequate and the PRD **closes with that finding** — current evidence says marginal (+0.573 vs +0.571, 17 fitted parameters adding ~nothing). Only if it fails: the exact rotamer master equation `M = L − diag(k)` with exchange generator **L measured from the shipped A48_C1R MD** (28,110 frames → reversible MSM, gated on reproducing the direct dipole autocorrelation), per-rotamer `k_i(d, χ)` on real conformer atoms with a Vaiana-style orientation factor, one global site-specific exchange-scaling exponent, then — as the point of the differentiable formulation — **backprop to the two physical fields**: the quenching surface `k(d, χ)` (does it rediscover π-stacking?) and the interaction potential that sets the rotamer weights (does learned stickiness localise to Lys/Arg, as Prakash measured?). Anisotropy follows without channels: `r∞/r0 = ⟨P2⟩` of the slowest mode; the static bound state (2026-08-22) becomes a theorem of the formulation. Surrogate/eigen/VV-VH machinery reused unchanged; a UDE in PRD-115's sense, not a collocation PINN (PRD-110 stopped that). First move: `s39_continuum_verdict.py` in `prototypes/quench_pinn/`.

* 📐 [PRD-121: FPS, natively — the C# FRET positioning and screening toolkit as programs in `bin/`](prd-121.md) — **status: proposed, plan only (2026-08-31).** FPS (Kalinin 2012) is the reference implementation of FRET-restrained rigid-body docking and of FRET screening; it is C# WinForms, Windows-only in practice, and its physics is almost entirely here already — AVs, asymmetric ±χ², `build_docking_assembly`, `dock_minimize` (whose own docstring calls itself "the FPS approach"), `refine_docking`, `screen_structures`. What is missing is **the product**, in seven pieces: a **project** binding structures + labelling + distances + per-mode parameters; **error estimation**, which in FPS is a *parametric bootstrap* — model distances taken as truth, each perturbed by its own asymmetric ±error, re-docked — and is **not** what `imp_bff dock-errors` measures; **screening diagnostics** (1/2/3σ violation counts, `InvalidR`, the per-structure R table, and RefRMSD via a Kabsch fit of per-position reference atoms), where `screen_structures` returns `(path, score)` and nothing else; **bond restraints** (a distance between two plain atoms drops both vdW radii to 0.4 Å and reports `Ebond` separately); a **results table and exports** (per-repetition E/E_clash/E_bond/converged/parent, PyMOL script, overlays, Rtable, chi2table); the **three tool dialogs** (distance calculator, conversion function, AV `.xyz`); and the **protocol details that change the answer** (Selected/All/SelectedThenAll, the clash-free shuffle, the force cap). Decisions taken: **IMP backend** — scores pinned, coordinates explicitly not; **five programs** in `bin/` mirroring FPS's own split; **both project formats** (the legacy `om.Export` text twin in, extended `.fps.json` in and out — the gzipped .NET `BinaryFormatter` `.bin` is a non-goal); **both input doors everywhere** (legacy positions+distances pair, or one fps.json), which puts `reference_atoms` into the schema at **1.2**. The A/B pin for screening is already in the tree: the Zenodo T4L set, 421 structures × 33 pairs of ⟨R_DA⟩ plus χ² for three states, produced by the real FPS. `AVS`/`EDF` are not ported — FPS parses them and computes neither. Open: whether a docking A/B reference exists at all.

* ✅ [PRD-120: the Labelizer, natively — a 1:1 port, A/B'd, and one container out](prd-120.md) — **status: done (2026-08-24).** The published label-site score (Gebhardt 2025) could not be evaluated on any machine here — `ss` shells out to DSSP and raises `RuntimeError("Unknown platform")` on macOS, `se` and `me` shell out to MSMS binaries that are 32-bit ppc/i386 Mach-O, and it pins the banned LabelLib — while `IMP.bff` already owned every piece of physics underneath it and had **no per-residue scoring layer at all**. Ported 1:1 as `ll_*`/`Ll*` across `LabelizerFeatures.h` (native Kabsch–Sander DSSP, half-sphere exposure, RSA, residue depth, ConSurf import), `LabelizerScore.h` (29 fitted likelihood tables, seven parameters, the weighted geometric mean) and `LabelizerFret.h` (pair scores, the analytic alpha cone, two-tier screen → AV refinement on the module's own AVs). **A/B against the reference's own published 1DDB output**: `ss` and `cr` reproduce it **exactly on all 195 residues** — the DSSP result needed two real corrections, the turn extent (`k+1…k+n-1`, not the donor) and **DSSP 3.0's reversal of π/α precedence** (Touw 2015) — `cs` is exact to sixteen digits, and `se` (native surface vs MSMS) has **no bias**: mean +0.015 Å, r = 0.976, 92.3 % within one table bin. Two findings worth carrying: the shipped 1DDB example **feeds its own conservation output back in as input**, and the lookup has a two-cycle, so its `cs` reference holds two distinct values across 195 residues and is not reproducible from any shipped file; and the analytic cone **over-reaches by 2–5 Å**, reordering the top five pairs once real dye clouds are built — `n_refine` matters. Published arithmetic is the **default, three defects included** (`LlModel`), each documented and correctable. Output is one `.mmfdb.pto` over PRD-118's `Pto.h`, through a **reusable** `PtoProfile.h` term layer; absence replaces the reference's `-1`/`0` sentinel collision. Drove the mmfdb 1.7 → 1.8 pass, which grew from the one term it needed (`row_grain = label_site`) to the vocabulary the fluorophore database had always been missing — `_mmfdb_optical_property.*`, `_mmfdb_spectrum.*`, `_mmfdb_probe.*` and seven attribution items — and gained `SolventAccessibleSurface.h` its first consumer plus a correct per-atom-radius variant. **Since shipping**: `DyeLibrary` became `ProbeLibrary` (a `Probe` is a dye, a fluorescent protein or a spin label; `forster_radius` now refuses a non-fluorescent one by naming the type), and the library CIF's `probe_type` column turned out to hold the *vendor* — wrong for as long as it existed, because nothing read it. [`okf/validation/labelizer_ab.md`](../validation/labelizer_ab.md).

* 🔨 [PRD-115: a differentiable lattice — adjoint of the field solver, per-voxel features, and a learned field on `MlpCore.h`](prd-115.md) — **status: active.** Built on two PRD-111 findings: `slow_factor`/`contact_distance` are one parameter (the data determine the mobility *field*, so re-parameterise on a smooth function of per-voxel features), and every gradient in the stack is a finite difference through a 1–10 s solve. * 💡 [PRD-114 (idea): an implicit-solvent AV — RISM potential of mean force × tethered-chain propagator](prd-114.md) — **status: idea, research only (2026-08-19), not started.** Today's AV is purely steric and the rotamer weights are vacuum LJ ± Debye–Hückel; nothing knows about water. Literal "RISM instead of AV" does not exist (RISM gives densities of free species; a tethered dye is neither free nor a solvent). The formulation that works: ρ_dye(r) ∝ G_linker(r|anchor)·exp(−βW(r)) — W(r) the solvent-averaged PMF of the free chromophore vs protein from RISM (Kast solute–solute RISM / 3D-RISM co-solvent "ligand mapping"), G_linker the Edwards propagator of the tether on the AV grid (a diffusion equation in contour length — the family bff already has). Steric AV is its hard-core, zero-entropy limit. Kast literature note (hybrid IE/MC 2002/2004, reduced models 2006, solute–solute PMFs 2016, free-energy densities 2017, nitroxide probes 2021/23) and a stage plan whose stage 0 is a stop gate: multiply one AmberTools W(r) into the existing AV/rotamer weights and see whether the PRD-108 AV↔rotamer table moves toward MD/experiment.

* ✅ [PRD-113: `IMP.bff` as a forward-model engine — restructure around Dye, Label, Representation, Dynamics, Photophysics](prd-113.md) — **status: done (2026-08-27).** `IMP.bff` is to structure what `tttrlib` is to photons: it emits **experiment-neutral** quantities (lifetime spectra, rate constants, κ² distributions, distances) and leaves convolution, IRF, pileup and fitting outside. The tree said otherwise — three unreconciled migrations left a whole TCSPC instrument layer with a single consumer, two AV builders over one C++ core, three distance layers, two κ² modules, two descriptions of a dye, `RotamerEnsemble(AccessibleVolume)` inheriting from the concrete AV class, and 42 numba kernels behind a shim that silently substitutes slow Python. Executed as in-place vertical slices, port-per-slice, because a flat C++ namespace cannot hold two `average_distance` — the port is what forces the de-duplication.

* 🔁 [PRD-111: what has to be measured — the quenching parameters are identifiable across sites, not within one](prd-111.md) — **status: needs re-measurement.** PRD-110 stopped because θ is not recoverable from one decay. θ is *global* (one dye, one chemistry, one mobility) while the geometry is *per site*, so N decays share one θ and the joint Fisher information is the **sum** — which beats its terms only if the blind directions rotate with geometry. Six T4L chain-A sites spanning quencher-in-contact (A124, TRP126 at 3.5 Å) to nearly-bare (A53): joint condition number **1.8 × 10⁵** against 1.96 × 10⁸ for the best single site and 1.43 × 10¹⁰ for A132. The rotation is the whole effect — joint `λ_min` 152.7 against 0.0456 for the sum of the per-site minima, a **3 350× gain**; second principal angles reach 88.4°. A joint fit confirms it: `free_diffusion` −52 % → **+2.5 %**, `kQ_scale` −69 % → **+4.3 %**, and in **97 evaluations against 676**. Two qualifications, both actionable: **`contact_distance` and `slow_factor` are one parameter, not two** — the fit reaches **reduced χ² = 1.016** at a `contact_distance` 33 % away from truth, reproducibly across resolutions, because `slow_factor^n(contact_distance)` builds the *same* mobility field (log D correlation 0.927, equilibrium overlap **98.7 %**); no number of sites separates them, `JᵀJ` error bars must not be quoted for either (the Fisher is confidently wrong about its *best*-determined parameter), and the model needs re-parameterising rather than more data. And the stated hypothesis was **wrong** — diffusion sensitivity tracks *quenched fraction*, not distance to quencher (corr −0.56): transport is rate-limiting when quenching depletes, so **to measure mobility, label where the dye is strongly quenched.** Repeated end to end at 2.0 Å with the same conclusion, so not a grid artefact. **Stage 1** perturbs the model two ways. A per-site free-dye fraction of 2–7 %, *unmodelled*, wipes out the gain (`free_diffusion` −60.5 %, `kQ_scale` −29.5 %) — but at reduced χ² 1.683, a **9.4 σ** rejection, so it announces itself; modelled (11 parameters) it restores χ² to 1.021 and brings `kQ_scale`/`rC`/`slow_factor` back to a few percent, with `free_diffusion` paying the cost at −13 % (a free fraction and fast diffusion both raise the long-time intensity). A **5 % linker-length error is benign** — `kQ_scale` −1.6 %, χ² 1.110 (1.5 σ) — the opposite of the expected answer: **worry about the sample, not the structure.** All data still synthetic from the model being fitted; fitting a measured decay is what remains. Found on the way and fixed: `compute_av` *wrote* `simulation_grid_resolution` into `source_info` from `disc_step` rather than reading it, so both quenching benchmarks' `--resolution` flag was inert and every run — including PRD-110's — was at the 1.5 Å default while the validation pages said 2.5 Å (numbers unaffected, label corrected); it now raises on disagreement (`test/fret/test_av_resolution.py`). `benchmark/kq_sensitivity_analysis.py`, [`okf/validation/quenching_multisite.md`](../validation/quenching_multisite.md).

* ⛔ [PRD-110: a parametric physics-informed surrogate for the quenching field](prd-110.md) — **status: stopped at the stage-0 gate (2026-08-17).** PRD-109's field solver costs 2.26 s per 50 ns at `ng = 41` and scales as `dg⁻⁵` (the explicit limit `dt ≤ dg²/6D`), which is fine once and ruinous in the loop that matters: the PET rates ship as *"starting values meant to be calibrated against measured lifetimes"*, and a 9-parameter calibration wants O(10²–10³) solves **per site**. Scopes a network trained on the PDE residual and parametric in θ, so calibration becomes a gradient walk over a differentiable surrogate. Faces the crux rather than deferring it — **a PINN suits a fixed domain with varying parameters, and every labelling site is a different domain**, so the scope is per-site and geometry conditioning is a separate (neural-operator) PRD. Stage 0 deliberately writes **no network**: it measures whether θ is identifiable from a single decay at all, since `kQ` and `slow_factor` plausibly trade off, and a faster fit to a degenerate problem is not progress — **if it is not identifiable the PRD stops there.** Stage 0 deliberately wrote **no network** and measured identifiability first: **condition number 1.4 × 10¹⁰**, and a real fit reproduced the data while missing `kQ_scale` by −69 % and `free_diffusion` by −52 %. A faster fit to a degenerate problem is not progress, so the PRD stopped and its four open decisions — including where a DL dependency could live — were never needed. Findings that outlived it: the equilibrium occupancy has the closed form `p ∝ 1/D` (verified to 1.1e-13, and the iteration it replaced was not converging), `slow_factor` compounds per contacting atom so it is only meaningful near 1.0, the decay becomes exactly D-independent at small `slow_factor`, and gradients need a θ-independent time step. [`okf/validation/quenching_identifiability.md`](../validation/quenching_identifiability.md).
